# Fi-art Codex v0.2

**Date:** June 25, 2026  
**Purpose:** Shareable source document for AI/product/design/legal/messaging teams building the Fi-art site and brand.

---

## 0. One-sentence definition

**Fi-art is a transparent rights marketplace where money-inspired art can remain art, become access, unlock real-world capacity, claim physical work, or, in restricted cases, redeem through approved fiat-liquidity rails.**

---

## 1. Core thesis

Money is already symbolic design: paper, ink, trust, law, memory, debt, labor, ritual, and belief. Fi-art makes that symbolic nature visible and useful.

Fi-art should not pretend all notes are money. It should show, with radical clarity, what each note actually is:

- **Open Fi-art:** art-only, unbacked, non-redeemable.
- **Capacity Notes:** redeemable for specific real-world goods, services, experiences, or inventory.
- **Art Notes:** claims to digital or physical artwork.
- **Access Notes:** claims to events, memberships, credentials, communities, or privileges.
- **Mirror Notes:** restricted fiat-redemption claims, only through approved reserve/liquidity rails.

The system’s job is to make value legible.

> **Art can be playful. Redemption must be exact.**

---

## 2. North star

**Fi-art educates people about money while giving them an off-ramp into real-world value.**

It is a living museum where the exhibits can be collected, gifted, traded, used, redeemed, or, in carefully structured cases, converted back into fiat.

---

## 3. Non-negotiable product doctrine

### 3.1 The sacred distinction

**Anyone can create Fi-art.  
Not anyone can create redeemable Fi-art.  
Only approved liquidity stewards can support fiat exits.**

### 3.2 Universal platform language

Use this everywhere:

> **Fi-art is not legal tender. Rights vary by note. Read the Truth Panel.**

Do **not** globally say:

> “Fi-art is not money.”

That is too broad, because some notes may have money-like redemption rights. Instead, state the specific rights of each note.

### 3.3 The Truth Panel rules everything

Every note must answer:

1. What is this?
2. Who made it?
3. What backs it, if anything?
4. Is it redeemable?
5. Redeemable for what?
6. Who must honor it?
7. Can it be sold or transferred?
8. Can it expire?
9. Can it become cash?
10. What can fail?

No note should be purchasable without a visible Truth Panel.

## 4. Note taxonomy

### 4.1 Open Fi-art

**Definition:** Money-inspired digital art. It may resemble, parody, abstract, remix, teach about, or aesthetically play with fiat. It is not backed by anything and has no redemption promise.

**What people pay for:** artistic value, collectibility, cultural commentary, humor, education, creator identity, beauty, provenance, scarcity, and optional resale potential.

**Truth Panel language:**

```text
Type: Open Fi-art
Redeemable: No
Backed by: Nothing
Reserve claim: None
Cash exit: No guaranteed cash exit
Value source: Artistic, cultural, educational, collectible
Risk label: Art-only
Exit path: Optional resale if another buyer exists
Legal status: Not legal tender
```

**Plain-language marketplace label:**

> **Art-only. Not redeemable. Not legal tender. No reserve claim.**

**Example:** A creator designs a surreal $1,000,000 “Breath Note” exploring attention as currency. Buyers purchase it as digital art, not because it is redeemable.

---

### 4.2 Capacity Notes

**Definition:** Notes redeemable for specific real-world capacity, such as hotel rooms, retreat seats, massages, dinners, events, workshops, studio sessions, private experiences, local services, or otherwise underused inventory.

**What people pay for:** the right to use or claim a specific good, service, experience, or time slot.

**Truth Panel language:**

```text
Type: Capacity Note
Redeemable: Yes
Redeemable for: [specific service / good / experience]
Cash redeemable: No, unless explicitly stated
Backed by: Provider capacity
Who honors it: [provider name]
Booking window: [dates]
Expiration: [date or none]
Transferable: [yes/no/restricted]
Risk label: Capacity-backed
Legal status: Not legal tender
```

**Plain-language marketplace label:**

> **Redeemable for listed capacity. Not cash unless explicitly stated. Not legal tender.**

**Example:** A hotel mints 20 weekday stay notes for underused rooms. A user buys one, gifts it, trades it, or redeems it for a stay.

---

### 4.3 Art Notes

**Definition:** Notes representing digital or physical artwork, including installations, prints, sculptural bills, oversized symbolic notes, or physical pieces that play with fiat imagery.

**What people pay for:** ownership, claim rights, provenance, collection value, and artistic meaning.

**Truth Panel language:**

```text
Type: Art Note
Redeemable: Maybe
Redeemable for: Digital art / physical art / installation / print / certificate
Cash redeemable: No, unless explicitly stated
Backed by: Artwork and creator promise
Who honors it: Creator / custodian / gallery
Risk label: Art-backed or creator-backed
Legal status: Not legal tender
```

**Plain-language marketplace label:**

> **Represents artwork or a claim to artwork. Not legal tender. Cash value not guaranteed.**

**Example:** A physical artwork incorporates ten visible $100 bills and auctions with a $1,000 reserve floor. The NFT represents a claim to the artwork, not a standing cash redemption promise.

---

### 4.4 Access Notes

**Definition:** Notes redeemable for membership, events, credentials, community access, private dinners, salons, courses, ceremonies, retreats, or privileges.

**Truth Panel language:**

```text
Type: Access Note
Redeemable: Yes
Redeemable for: [event / membership / access / credential]
Cash redeemable: No
Backed by: Platform or creator access promise
Who honors it: [platform / host / creator]
Expiration: [date / membership term]
Risk label: Access-backed
Legal status: Not legal tender
```

**Plain-language marketplace label:**

> **Redeemable for listed access. Not cash. Not legal tender.**

---

### 4.5 Mirror Notes

**Definition:** Restricted notes tied to fiat-redemption rights through approved reserve holders, art holders, liquidity stewards, custodians, or regulated partners.

**This is the most sensitive product category.** It should not be launched casually.

**Truth Panel language:**

```text
Type: Mirror Note
Redeemable: Yes
Redeemable for: Fiat / cash / equivalent, subject to terms
Cash redeemable: Yes, if eligible
Backed by: Verified reserve or approved liquidity steward
Who honors it: [approved entity]
Reserve status: [coverage / last verification]
Fees: [fees]
Limits: [limits]
Eligibility: [KYC/KYB/jurisdiction]
Risk label: Reserve-backed or restricted redemption claim
Legal status: Not legal tender
```

**Plain-language marketplace label:**

> **Restricted redemption claim. Not legal tender. Subject to reserve terms, eligibility, limits, fees, and approved redemption partner.**

## 5. Marketplace Credit naming

Avoid calling the platform unit simply “Fiart Credit” if it behaves like money.

Preferred name:

> **Fiart Marketplace Credit**

Definition:

> **Closed-loop platform credit usable only for eligible Fi-art listings and capacity offers. Not legal tender. Not cash. Not redeemable for fiat unless explicitly stated through an approved redemption path.**

Messaging rule:

- Do not say “1 Fiart = $1” unless the structure is ready for money-transmission/stablecoin scrutiny.
- Do not promise broad cash redemption from marketplace credits.
- Prefer specific note-level redemption rights over universal balance redemption.

## 9. Vouch system

Vouches are powerful but must not blur into fake insurance.

### Vouch types

| Type | Counts as backing? | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Social Vouch | No | “I trust this creator/provider.” |
| Funded Vouch / Collateral Pledge | Yes, limited | “I locked value to support this obligation.” |
| Capacity Vouch | Sometimes | “I can provide replacement service/capacity.” |
| Reserve Vouch | Yes, if verified | “I hold verified redemption liquidity.” |

### Language rules

Say:

```text
Social vouch
Collateral pledge
Reserve support
Capacity replacement support
```

Avoid:

```text
Insured
Guaranteed
Risk-free
Protected
Community insurance
Default coverage
```

### Public display example

```text
Social vouches: 18
Collateral pledges: $4,500 locked through Sep 30, 2026
Reserve support: $10,000 verified by Art Holder A
Capacity replacement vouches: 3 providers
```

## 10. Creator doctrine

### Creator covenant

Every creator should accept:

```text
I created this art or have rights to use it.
I am responsible for my artwork, claims, and redemption promises.
I will not create counterfeit-like currency.
I will not imply government endorsement.
I will not call Fi-art legal tender.
I will not promise cash redemption unless approved.
I will not promise profits, yield, or appreciation.
I understand my note may be delisted, frozen, marked impaired, or removed if claims are false or fulfillment fails.
```

### Open creator rule

Open Creators can make art-only notes. They cannot attach redemption rights without approval.

### Redeemable creator rule

Redeemable Creators must define:

```text
What is redeemable
Who honors it
How it is redeemed
When it expires
What happens if redemption fails
Whether it is transferable
Whether it can become cash
```

## 19. Legal/compliance anchors for builders

These are not marketing points. They are build constraints.

1. **Legal tender:** U.S. coins and currency, including Federal Reserve notes, are legal tender for debts, public charges, taxes, and dues. Fi-art should not call private notes legal tender.  
2. **Currency image use:** U.S. currency imagery has strict reproduction conditions, including one-sided use and size rules for color illustrations.  
3. **Money transmission / CVC:** Administrators or exchangers of convertible virtual currency may be treated as money transmitters depending on activities and exemptions.  
4. **Gift card / prepaid access:** Capacity-style notes may implicate prepaid/gift-card style rules, especially around expiration, fees, disclosures, and closed-loop use.  
5. **Advertising:** Claims should be truthful, non-deceptive, fair, and evidence-based.  
6. **Copyright:** User-generated art requires creator attestations, DMCA agent process, takedowns, and repeat-infringer policy.  
7. **Tax:** Digital assets, including NFTs, are generally treated as property for U.S. tax purposes.

Source anchors:

- 31 U.S.C. § 5103 legal tender: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/31/5103
- BEP currency image use: https://www.bep.gov/currency/currency-image-use
- FinCEN CVC guidance: https://www.fincen.gov/resources/statutes-regulations/guidance/application-fincens-regulations-persons-administering
- CFPB Regulation E gift card rules: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1005/20
- FTC advertising basics: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing
- U.S. Copyright Office DMCA agent directory: https://www.copyright.gov/dmca-directory/
- IRS digital assets: https://www.irs.gov/filing/digital-assets
