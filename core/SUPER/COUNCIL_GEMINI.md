# Gemini — independent council answer (NBM #11)
Fetched 2026-09-03 via API by Ember. Gemini saw STRATEGY/REDEMPTION/BUILD_001/MORE_ALIVE/NBM only — not the Claude/GPT critiques.

As an independent council member, I have reviewed the provided materials.

**(1) Single Strongest Objection**

My strongest objection is to the “MORE ALIVE” framework as the primary success metric (Q). It replaces a clear, falsifiable test of economic viability with a subjective, unfalsifiable test of spiritual or social resonance. A currency can be functionally dead (R_co = 1, vendor immediately redeems and refuses to participate again) while still allowing the project team to claim success because a participant reported an increase in “Meaning” or “Love.” This conflates a social experiment with a currency experiment and creates a dangerous blind spot. The project's own test question is “Can SUPER carry the loop on its own weight?”; MORE ALIVE does not answer this.

**(2) Three Most Likely Failure Modes of Build #001**

1.  **Provider Rejection due to Complexity:** The soda owner, a practical small businessperson, rejects the offer not out of malice but confusion. The 5% redemption fee, designed to encourage circulation, is perceived as a hidden cost or a trick. The explanation (“gastar SUPER con otra persona vale el 100%”) is meaningless when there is no one else to spend it with. The vendor concludes it’s easier and safer to deal in colones and politely declines.

2.  **Social Friction and Spectacle:** The experiment is perceived by beneficiaries and the community not as a “shared meal” but as a bizarre spectacle orchestrated by outsiders. Ten people are singled out to be fed with “tech money.” The process feels paternalistic, and the follow-up questions from the “MORE ALIVE” framework feel intrusive and clinical. The result is a loss of trust and dignity, regardless of whether the food was good.

3.  **Redemption Lag Kills Trust:** The provider accepts the 100 SUPER, provides ₡40,000 worth of food, and immediately requests redemption to buy more ingredients. The promised “within 7 days” is too slow for a small business operating on tight cash flow. Any delay, logistical hiccup, or perceived hesitation in delivering the gift card within hours, let alone days, permanently destroys the project’s credibility with the single most important person in this loop. The experiment technically succeeds but creates an enemy, guaranteeing Build #002 never happens.

**(3) One Thing This Design Gets Right**

The 5% redemption fee is brilliant. While a potential failure point in Build #001, it is the correct long-term design choice. Most complementary currency projects fail because their token is just a inconvenient proxy for national currency, with no penalty for immediate cash-out. By making redemption slightly worse than spending, the design creates a clear, structural incentive for circulation (R_co > 1) from day one. It directly addresses the core problem of velocity, which similar projects ignore.

**(4) Verdict**

**Proceed with one change.**

The change: For Build #001, replace the “MORE ALIVE” framework as the arbiter of success (Q). The pass/fail criteria (Q) must be concrete and directly answer the build’s test question. It should be a simple checklist:
1.  Provider understood and freely accepted SUPER.
2.  Beneficiaries received a meal they wanted and felt respected.
3.  Provider redemption request was honored frictionlessly within 24 hours.
4.  Provider states they would accept SUPER again under these terms.

If all four are “yes,” the build succeeds. The MORE ALIVE questions can be asked as a qualitative follow-up for learning, but they are not the grade. Focus on proving the mechanics work before measuring the meaning.
