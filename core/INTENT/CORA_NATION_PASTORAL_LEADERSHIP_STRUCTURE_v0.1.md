## 1. The Bifurcated Authority Principle

CORA Nation operates under **Bifurcated Authority**, a structural separation between two distinct domains of governance:

| Domain | Authority | Held by | Subject to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spiritual / Doctrinal / Pastoral** | Religious-community character; doctrine; ceremony; pastoral care; discipline; recognition of Stewards | Founding Steward + Pastoral Council | Member supermajority on doctrinal amendments; Founding Steward veto on doctrinal matters (overridable by ¾ Member supermajority) |
| **Secular Fiduciary** | Asset disposition; Steward compensation; budget; counsel engagement; Brand LLC arm's-length agreements | Stewardship Council (majority independent) | Member supermajority on supermajority matters; standard majority otherwise; threshold-gated allocations |

Per Treasury v0.10:

> *"Private inurement is the number one cause of intentional religious community legal destruction... Architectural separation between spiritual authority and secular fiduciary control is the structural defense."*

This document specifies the Spiritual / Doctrinal / Pastoral structure. The Secular Fiduciary structure (Stewardship Council) is specified in Bylaws Article VII.

## 2. The Founding Steward

### 2.1 Identity and Founding Authority
The Founding Steward of CORA Nation is **James Sunheart Stinson**, who founded the religious community, authored or compiled the foundational canonical documents, and continues to hold spiritual / doctrinal / pastoral authority per Article III of the Bylaws.

### 2.2 Scope of Authority
The Founding Steward's authority is **spiritual, doctrinal, and pastoral** — not fiduciary. The Founding Steward:

a) Interprets and articulates the doctrinal core of CORA Nation;
b) Convenes and chairs the Pastoral Council;
c) Recognizes (ordains/commissions) new Stewards in concert with the Pastoral Council;
d) Conducts and oversees ceremony, sacrament, and Mirror Witnessing practice;
e) Provides pastoral care directly and through delegated Stewards;
f) Exercises the doctrinal veto per Bylaws §5.07;
g) Initiates Canon amendments per Bylaws Article VIII §8.03.

### 2.3 Limits on Authority
The Founding Steward does **not**:

a) Hold unilateral voting authority over disposition, sale, or distribution of any Community-held or Trust-held assets (Bylaws §3.03);
b) Set the Founding Steward's own compensation (subject to §4958 procedure per Conflict of Interest Policy);
c) Override the Stewardship Council on secular fiduciary matters;
d) Vote on supermajority secular matters with greater weight than any other Member;
e) Hold the Office of Treasurer (Bylaws Article IX);
f) Be a counted Independent member of the Stewardship Council.

### 2.4 Compensation
Compensation to the Founding Steward in the form of community-provision is subject to the §4958 Rebuttable-Presumption procedure: (i) comparable-compensation data from peer religious organizations; (ii) approval by Stewardship Council members who are not subject to recusal under the Conflict of Interest Policy; (iii) contemporaneous written documentation. No community-provision to the Founding Steward shall be authorized except per this procedure.

**Founding-Stage Activation Gate (cross-reference Bylaws §12.03):** No community-provision compensation to the Founding Steward shall be authorized until (a) at least three (3) independent Stewardship Council members have been seated with documented independence per Bylaws Article III §3.04, (b) those Stewardship Council members have completed initial COI disclosures per Conflict of Interest Policy §4.1, and (c) the §4958 procedure has been completed and contemporaneously documented for the relevant compensation determination. This gate is a hard-rule under Bylaws §12.02 and may not be waived.

The Founding Steward may receive **non-community-provision** compensation from a Brand LLC for services rendered to that LLC on arm's-length terms, governed by the Brand LLC's own decision-making and the Conflict of Interest Policy (recusal on any Community decision affecting that LLC).

### 2.5 Succession on Incapacity or Death
Upon the Founding Steward's incapacity, resignation, or death:

a) The Pastoral Council assumes the Founding Steward's spiritual / doctrinal / pastoral authority by collective decision;
b) A **Successor Steward** may be designated by the Founding Steward in advance (in a written designation maintained with Community records) and ratified by Pastoral Council unanimous vote;
c) If no Successor Steward is designated, the Pastoral Council shall convene a successor-selection process per Canon and shall present the proposed Successor Steward for Member supermajority ratification per Bylaws §5.03;
d) During any vacancy in the Founding Steward role, the Pastoral Council acts collectively in matters that would otherwise require the Founding Steward's individual decision.

### 2.6 Resignation
The Founding Steward may resign from the Founding Steward role by written notice to the Pastoral Council, the Stewardship Council, and the Membership. Resignation takes effect on a date specified in the notice (not less than thirty (30) days after notice) and triggers the succession process in §2.5.

### 2.7 Removal
The Founding Steward may be removed only for cause as defined in the Conflict of Interest Policy or for sustained, documented violation of the Coherent Champions Manifesto or these Bylaws, by joint action of:

a) The entire Pastoral Council excluding the Founding Steward, subject to §2.7(b);

b) The Stewardship Council acting by supermajority;

c) **Member supermajority sized by Community scale:** two-thirds (2/3) of all Members (not merely those voting) when CORA Nation has fewer than fifty (50) Members; three-fourths (3/4) of all Members when CORA Nation has fifty (50) or more Members.

**§2.7(a) Initial Pastoral Council Ratification Gate.** Pastoral Members who were initially appointed by the Founding Steward at the time of v1.0 adoption per §3.1 and have not yet been independently ratified by Member supermajority per Bylaws §5.03 at the first Annual Meeting are NOT eligible to vote on the removal of the Founding Steward. Removal of the Founding Steward may only be initiated after Member ratification of the Pastoral Council per Bylaws §14.02. Until such ratification, the Pastoral Council may signal pastoral concern, refer the matter to the Stewardship Council, or initiate other processes short of removal.

**§2.7(b) Rationale for Sunset.** The two-tier removal threshold reflects the asymmetric reality of religious-community founding: when the Community is small (fewer than 50 Members), the founder's role is foundational and protection against capricious removal serves the Community's stability; when the Community is established at scale, broader Member voice is appropriate. The threshold is not intended to make removal impossible, and the Member-ratification gate in §2.7(a) prevents the founder from creating a Pastoral Council that cannot remove the founder.

## 3. The Pastoral Council

### 3.1 Composition
The Pastoral Council consists of the Founding Steward and not fewer than two (2) additional individuals (the "**Pastoral Members**"), recognized by the Founding Steward and ratified by Member supermajority per Bylaws §5.03.

Initial Pastoral Members shall be appointed by the Founding Steward at the time of v1.0 adoption of this document, subject to ratification at the first Annual Meeting following counsel sign-off.

### 3.2 Eligibility
A Pastoral Member shall be:

a) A Member in good standing for not less than one (1) year (waived for initial appointments);
b) Of demonstrated religious-community commitment, character, and pastoral discernment;
c) Recognized by the Founding Steward and accepted by the Pastoral Council;
d) Free of conflicts of interest that would prevent independent pastoral judgment (per the Conflict of Interest Policy).

### 3.3 Term
Pastoral Members serve indeterminate terms subject to good standing, voluntary resignation, or removal per §3.6.

### 3.4 Authority
The Pastoral Council exercises the spiritual / doctrinal / pastoral authority of CORA Nation per Bylaws Article VIII, including:

a) Concurrence with the Founding Steward on doctrinal amendments;
b) Discipline and restoration of Members;
c) Recognition (ordination/commissioning) of Stewards (§4);
d) Removal of Stewards (jointly with the Stewardship Council);
e) Oversight of ceremony, sacrament, and pastoral care practice;
f) Resolution of conflicts at the doctrinal/fiduciary boundary jointly with the Stewardship Council.

### 3.5 Procedure
The Pastoral Council meets at least quarterly, in person or remotely. Quorum is a majority of total Pastoral Members. Decisions are by majority of those present, except as otherwise specified (e.g., Founding Steward succession in §2.5 requires unanimity).

### 3.6 Resignation and Removal
A Pastoral Member may resign at any time by written notice to the Founding Steward. A Pastoral Member may be removed for cause by joint action of the Founding Steward and a supermajority of the remaining Pastoral Members.

## 4. Stewards

### 4.1 Steward Role
A **Steward** is a Member who has been recognized by CORA Nation for a defined pastoral, operational, or fiduciary role. Stewards exercise authority within their specific role per these Bylaws, this Pastoral Leadership Structure, and any role-specific charter.

### 4.2 Types of Stewards
Stewards include but are not limited to:

| Steward type | Role | Authority domain |
|---|---|---|
| **Operating Stewards** | Lead a Working Circle (e.g., Camp Zen Circle, Brain Circle, Land Circle) | Day-to-day operations of that Circle; allocation authority within Circle scope per Bylaws |
| **Pastoral Stewards** | Provide pastoral care, witnessing, or teaching | Pastoral practice; sacramental functions; Mirror Witnessing |
| **Fiduciary Stewards** | Sit on the Stewardship Council | Per Bylaws Article VII |
| **Specialty Stewards** (Treasurer, Counsel Liaison, Secretary, etc.) | Per Bylaws Article IX | Per Bylaws Article IX |

A Member may hold multiple Steward roles subject to the Conflict of Interest Policy.

### 4.3 Recognition Process
A Member is recognized as a Steward through the following process:

a) **Discernment** — the Pastoral Council and the Member (and where relevant, the Stewardship Council for fiduciary roles) engage in extended pastoral discernment, typically not less than ninety (90) days, including practice of the proposed role with feedback;

b) **Recommendation** — the Pastoral Council recommends recognition to the Membership;

c) **Ratification** — where the Steward role carries fiduciary authority, Member ratification per Bylaws §5.02 is required; for purely pastoral roles, no Member vote is required but the recognition is announced at a Member meeting;

d) **Recognition Ceremony** — the recognition is solemnized in a Community ceremony (commissioning, ordination, or analogous) per Canon practice;

e) **Conflict of Interest Disclosure** — the Steward completes initial Conflict of Interest disclosure per the COI Policy before assuming Steward duties.

### 4.4 Steward Conduct
Stewards are bound by:

a) The Bylaws, this Pastoral Leadership Structure, and the Conflict of Interest Policy;
b) Any role-specific charter or commission;
c) The pastoral and doctrinal authority of the Pastoral Council;
d) The Coherent Champions Manifesto governing principles.

### 4.5 Steward Compensation
A Steward serving in an operational or fiduciary role may receive **community-provision compensation** per Treasury v0.10's community-provision model, subject to §4958 Rebuttable-Presumption procedure per the COI Policy. A Steward serving in a purely pastoral or ceremonial role typically serves without compensation.

A Steward who simultaneously owns or operates a Brand LLC may receive **arm's-length commercial compensation** from that Brand LLC, subject to recusal from any Community decision affecting that Brand LLC.

### 4.6 Steward Removal
A Steward may be removed:

a) For cause as defined in the COI Policy, by joint action of the Pastoral Council and a majority of the Stewardship Council;
b) By Member supermajority per Bylaws §5.03(c);
c) By voluntary resignation, effective on a date specified in written notice.

### 4.7 Steward Restoration
A removed Steward may be restored to Steward standing through the recognition process in §4.3, with the period of disqualification and any conditions specified by the Pastoral Council.

## 5. Pastoral Care Practice

### 5.1 Mirror Witnessing
Mirror Witnessing per the Mirror Constitution v1.0 is the core pastoral practice of CORA Nation. Each Member is expected to enter a Mirror Witnessing relationship within ninety (90) days of completing Apprenticeship, paired with a Distance-Weighted Witness per the §4.5 protocol of the Mirror Constitution.

### 5.2 Pastoral Counseling
The Pastoral Council and Pastoral Stewards provide pastoral counseling to Members. Pastoral communications are confidential per the Records & Privacy Protocol and applicable clergy-penitent privilege; the Pastoral Council shall not be required to disclose the substance of pastoral communications except as required by law or with the consenting Member's express written authorization.

### 5.3 Ceremony and Sacrament
The Pastoral Council oversees the ceremonial and sacramental life of CORA Nation per Canon practice, including Ecstatic Weekend, A*Zen*tion, Saturday Casual, Coherence Course, witnessing ceremonies, Steward recognition ceremonies, and any successor practices recognized by the Pastoral Council.

### 5.4 Doctrine and Teaching
The Pastoral Council oversees the teaching and dissemination of Canon and may delegate teaching authority to Pastoral Stewards or other qualified Members. Members may teach within the Community subject to Pastoral Council oversight.

## 6. Discipline and Restoration

### 6.1 Pastoral Discipline
The Pastoral Council exercises discipline of Members for material violations of the Coherent Champions Manifesto, the Bylaws, this Structure, or the Conflict of Interest Policy. Discipline is restorative in intent.

### 6.2 Process
A Member subject to discipline shall receive:

a) Written notice of the conduct at issue;
b) Opportunity to respond in writing or in person before the Pastoral Council;
c) Pastoral mediation prior to any formal discipline decision;
d) A decision in writing setting forth the basis for discipline and any conditions for restoration;
e) The right to appeal to the Pastoral Council in plenary session if not previously addressed in plenary.

### 6.3 Outcomes
Discipline outcomes may include:

a) Pastoral counsel and accompaniment;
b) Temporary suspension of Member voting rights;
c) Temporary suspension of Steward role (if applicable);
d) Termination of Membership in cases of sustained, documented unwillingness to walk in the Community's covenant;
e) Such other outcomes as the Pastoral Council determines consonant with restorative discipline.

### 6.4 Restoration
A disciplined Member may seek restoration per the path specified in the discipline decision, typically including pastoral accompaniment, demonstrated change of practice, and (where relevant) restitution.

## 7. Coordination with Stewardship Council

The Pastoral Council and the Stewardship Council are distinct but coordinate on matters that cross the doctrinal/fiduciary boundary. Coordination includes:

a) **Joint meetings** — at least annually, the Pastoral Council and Stewardship Council meet jointly to review the state of the Community;
b) **Steward recognition** — fiduciary Stewards are recognized jointly per §4.3;
c) **Steward removal** — Stewards holding fiduciary roles are removed jointly per §4.6;
d) **Boundary disputes** — matters that may be characterized as either doctrinal or fiduciary (e.g., compensation of pastoral Stewards) are resolved by joint determination of both Councils, with a tie broken by a Member vote per Bylaws §5.02;
e) **Conflict of Interest determinations** affecting a Pastoral or Stewardship Council member are made by the other Council.
