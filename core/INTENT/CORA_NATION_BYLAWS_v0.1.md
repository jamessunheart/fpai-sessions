## ARTICLE I — Name, Purpose, Form

### §1.01 Name
The name of the religious community is **CORA Nation** (also known as "Community Orchestrated Resource Abundance Nation"; "CORA"; or "the Community").

### §1.02 Form
CORA Nation is an unincorporated religious association ("ecclesial association" or "religious membership association") **domiciled in the State of Florida** (where its existence is evidenced by notarized founding declarations), self-declared under Internal Revenue Code § 508(c)(1)(A) as an organization not required to apply for tax-exempt recognition because of its character as a church or religious community.

CORA Nation operates as a **Private Membership Association (PMA)** under the First Amendment freedom of association, the Florida Religious Freedom Restoration Act (F.S. 761.01 et seq.), the Florida Church Property Act, and common-law association doctrine, conducting intra-member religious, spiritual, pastoral, ceremonial, educational, and community-economic activity.

These Bylaws and all matters arising under or related to CORA Nation's internal governance are governed by the laws of the State of Florida, without regard to conflicts-of-law principles, subject to the federal First Amendment and IRC protections that govern religious-community associations. To the extent CORA Nation maintains operations in Costa Rica (the Zen Village substrate), Costa Rica law governs solely the employment, residency, and tax matters arising from those operations.

### §1.03 Purpose
The religious purpose of CORA Nation is:

a) The proclamation and practice of the Coherent Champions Manifesto and the World Peace Agreement covenant;

b) The discipleship of Champions and Stewards toward visible human flourishing in coherence with the divine source named in the Canon;

c) The ceremonial, sacramental, pastoral, and witnessing practices specified in Canon v0.1 (Mirror Witnessing, Character Card discernment, Ecstatic Weekend, A*Zen*tion, Saturday Casual, Coherence Course, and successor practices);

d) The orchestration of resource abundance among Members as a religious-economic discipline ("Community Orchestrated Resource Abundance"), conducted in obedience to the doctrines compiled in Canon v0.1;

e) The stewardship of physical, financial, and informational resources entrusted to the Community for the religious mission, including the Zen Village substrate in Costa Rica;

f) The publication and operationalization of religious-community canon, ceremony, and pastoral care.

### §1.04 Principal Office and Substrate
The principal religious-community substrate of CORA Nation is the **Zen Village** site in Costa Rica (45 acres, Chirripó region). Records and electronic operations may be maintained at locations designated by the Stewardship Council from time to time.

### §1.05 No Profit Motive
CORA Nation conducts no commercial enterprise as its primary or substantial activity. Commercial activity by Members or related entities is conducted by separate for-profit entities (Brand LLCs) at arm's-length from CORA Nation per the Brand Tokenization Architecture v0.4 CONVERGED.

## ARTICLE II — Membership

### §2.01 Eligibility
Membership in CORA Nation is open to any natural person who:

a) Affirms the Coherent Champions Manifesto and the World Peace Agreement;

b) Completes the Admission Procedures (Constitutional Pack Document #5);

c) Signs the PMA Membership Agreement (Constitutional Pack Document #3);

d) Receives recognition as a Member by the existing Membership per the Admission Procedures.

### §2.02 Membership Tiers
The Community recognizes the following Membership tiers, each with the scope of religious-community participation and governance voice specified in these Bylaws:

| Tier | Religious-community status | Bylaws voice |
|---|---|---|
| **Inquirer** | Has signed the World Peace Agreement; is exploring membership | No vote in Member meetings |
| **Apprentice** | Has signed PMA Membership Agreement; is in formation per Admission Procedures (typically 90 days minimum) | Vote on local-Circle matters per §2.05; no vote in Community-wide matters |
| **Member** | Has completed Apprenticeship; recognized by Community-process | Full vote in Community-wide matters |
| **Steward** | Member with formal pastoral, operational, or fiduciary role per Pastoral Leadership Structure | Member voting rights + Stewardship duties; conflict-of-interest recusal applies (Article VI) |
| **Elder** | Steward emeritus or long-serving Member recognized by the Stewardship Council | Full Member rights; pastoral counsel function |

### §2.03 Religious Character of Membership
Membership in CORA Nation is a religious commitment, not an investment, partnership, contractual exchange, or commercial relationship. Membership confers no economic ownership, no transferable property interest, no expectation of monetary return, and no entitlement to commercial benefits from any Brand LLC or related entity.

### §2.04 No Transfer of Membership
Membership is personal and may not be transferred, sold, assigned, inherited, or otherwise alienated. Membership terminates per Article II of the Admission & Exit Procedures (Constitutional Pack Document #5).

### §2.05 Working Circles
Members may be organized into **Working Circles** corresponding to ministries, domains, or geographies of religious-community life (e.g., Camp Zen Circle, Brain Circle, Land Circle, Mockumentary Circle). Each Circle has scoped allocation authority within its domain as further defined in these Bylaws and the Allocation Platform spec (when ratified).

## ARTICLE III — Bifurcated Authority

CORA Nation operates under the **Bifurcated Authority** principle required by Remarkably Coherent Treasury v0.10 §Governance Firewall: spiritual / doctrinal / pastoral authority is structurally separated from secular fiduciary authority.

### §3.01 Spiritual / Doctrinal / Pastoral Authority
Spiritual, doctrinal, and pastoral authority is held by the **Founding Steward** (currently James Sunheart Stinson) and the **Pastoral Council** as constituted in the Pastoral Leadership Structure (Constitutional Pack Document #4). This authority covers:

a) Interpretation and amendment of religious doctrine (Canon evolution);
b) Recognition and ordination of Stewards;
c) Conduct of ceremony, sacrament, and witnessing practice;
d) Pastoral care of Members;
e) Discipline and restoration of Members per Admission & Exit Procedures.

### §3.02 Secular Fiduciary Authority
Secular fiduciary authority over Community resources is held by the **Stewardship Council** as constituted in the Pastoral Leadership Structure. This authority covers:

a) Custody and disposition of Community assets;
b) Determination of Steward compensation under the community-provision model (subject to Article VI Conflict of Interest);
c) Budget approval and annual allocation;
d) Engagement of counsel, accountants, and other professional advisors;
e) Approval of Brand LLC arm's-length agreements where CORA Nation is a counterparty;
f) Approval of any allocation above thresholds set in §5.04.

### §3.03 Non-overlap Principle
The Founding Steward does **not** hold unilateral voting authority over disposition, sale, or dividend distribution of donated assets held by the Sunheart Private Trust or other CORA-affiliated fiduciary vehicles. The Stewardship Council holds majority fiduciary control over those specific asset classes, per Treasury v0.10 §Governance Firewall and the Sunheart Private Trust instrument (as amended per Brand Tok v0.4 activation preconditions).

### §3.04 Independent Members of the Stewardship Council
At least a majority of Stewardship Council members at any time must be **independent** of any single Brand LLC ownership interest and not in close personal relationship (familial, romantic, or business-partnership) with the Founding Steward. Independence is determined annually per the Conflict of Interest Policy (Constitutional Pack Document #6).

## ARTICLE IV — Meetings of Members

### §4.01 Annual Meeting
The Community holds an Annual Meeting of Members in person at the Zen Village (or by hybrid in-person/remote means as designated by the Stewardship Council) within thirty (30) days of the anniversary of CORA Nation's founding (date set per Pastoral Council resolution).

### §4.02 Regular Member Gatherings
Regular Member gatherings (Ecstatic Weekend, A*Zen*tion, Saturday Casual, Coherence Course, and successor practices per Canon) constitute religious-community life. The Stewardship Council may designate specific gatherings as **decision-making sessions** for matters within Member voice per Article V.

### §4.03 Special Meetings
Special meetings of Members may be called by:

a) The Founding Steward;
b) Two-thirds (2/3) of the Stewardship Council;
c) Written request of one-third (1/3) of Members.

### §4.04 Notice
Notice of any meeting at which a Member decision is to be taken shall be provided to all Members not less than ten (10) days in advance through the Community's designated communication channels. The notice shall include the agenda items, the proposed decisions, and the supporting materials.

### §4.05 Quorum
A quorum for any decision-making Member meeting is **the lesser of (i) one-third (1/3) of all Members or (ii) twenty-one (21) Members.** Decisions taken without quorum are advisory only.

### §4.06 Remote Participation and Asynchronous Decisions
Members may participate in any meeting and may cast votes through the Community's designated electronic systems, provided that records of participation and votes are retained per the Records & Privacy Protocol (Constitutional Pack Document #7).

Decisions on matters that do not require simultaneous deliberation (per §5.03) may be taken asynchronously over a defined voting window (minimum 7 days, maximum 30 days), recorded in the system.

## ARTICLE V — Member Decisions

### §5.01 Scope of Member Voice
Members exercise governance voice on the following matters:

a) Amendment of these Bylaws (subject to §12.02 limits);
b) Amendment of the Canon (subject to Pastoral Council scope per Article III);
c) Recognition of new Stewards (joint with Pastoral Council per Pastoral Leadership Structure);
d) Removal of Stewards (joint with Pastoral Council);
e) Annual ratification of the Stewardship Council;
f) Approval of any allocation exceeding the thresholds in §5.04;
g) Adoption of religious-community policies of general application;
h) Other matters explicitly committed to the Membership by these Bylaws or by Stewardship Council referral.

### §5.02 Standard Decision Rule
Except as otherwise specified, Member decisions are taken by **simple majority of those voting** in the quorum window, with all votes weighted equally by Member (one Member, one vote, regardless of accumulated Allocation Weight or any other measure).

### §5.03 Supermajority Decisions
The following decisions require **two-thirds (2/3) approval of those voting in the quorum window:**

a) Amendment of these Bylaws;
b) Amendment of the Canon;
c) Removal of a Steward;
d) Approval of any flow from Community treasury to a Brand LLC (regardless of size);
e) Approval of dissolution (further restricted by Article XIII).

### §5.04 Allocation Thresholds
Any single allocation of Community resources exceeding **the greater of (i) ten percent (10%) of unrestricted Community assets at the start of the fiscal year or (ii) USD $25,000 (as adjusted under §5.04(b))** requires Member approval per §5.02 or §5.03 (as applicable). Allocations below this threshold may be approved by the Stewardship Council subject to Article VI Conflict of Interest.

**§5.04(a) Routine-Operations Carve-Out.** Notwithstanding the foregoing, allocations within categories that have been pre-approved by the Stewardship Council in the annual budget (e.g., ceremony costs, land maintenance, Steward community-provision within §4958-approved ranges, routine professional fees, member-gathering operational costs) may be expended without separate Member approval, subject to (i) each category having a documented annual cap; (ii) the cumulative annual expenditure in each category not exceeding its cap; and (iii) the Treasurer reporting category expenditure to the Stewardship Council quarterly and to the Membership at the Annual Meeting.

**§5.04(b) Inflation Indexing.** The USD $25,000 threshold in §5.04 shall be adjusted annually by the Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U), rounded to the nearest USD $1,000, effective at the start of each fiscal year. The Treasurer shall report the current threshold to the Membership at each Annual Meeting.

### §5.05 Allocation Platform Integration
When and if the Allocation Platform (Constitutional Pack downstream artifact) is ratified by Member supermajority per §5.03(a), voting under §5.02 and §5.03 may be conducted through the Allocation Platform per the rules of that spec. The Platform's structural rules (no transfer endpoint, no purchase endpoint, recusal auto-enforcement, etc.) operate as **protocol-layer constants** and become hard-rules under §12.02 **upon and from the date of Member ratification of the Allocation Platform spec**.

Until such Member ratification, §5.05 establishes the structural intent of the Allocation Platform layer but does not yet incorporate any specific protocol-layer constants. The Bylaws ratified at the first Annual Meeting commit Members to the Bylaws structure shown here; specific Allocation Platform constants will be ratified separately in a future Member supermajority vote following counsel sign-off on the Allocation Platform spec.

### §5.06 Concentration Limit
No Member, including through delegated proxy, may exercise more than **twenty-five percent (25%) of total votes cast** on any single Member decision. The Allocation Platform shall enforce this limit at the protocol layer. Delegation chains are limited to one (1) hop; no proxy-of-proxy is permitted.

### §5.07 Founding Steward Veto (Doctrinal Matters Only)
The Founding Steward retains a veto over Member decisions on Canon amendments or doctrinal matters under Article III §3.01 spiritual / doctrinal / pastoral authority. This veto does **not** extend to secular fiduciary matters, asset disposition, Steward compensation, or any matter within Stewardship Council authority under Article III §3.02.

**Definition of "doctrinal matters" for purposes of this veto.** "Doctrinal matters" means matters directly concerning:

a) The interpretation, amendment, or operationalization of Canon;
b) The conduct of ceremony, sacrament, and Mirror Witnessing practice;
c) The recognition, ordination, or removal of persons from pastoral roles;
d) Doctrinal teaching authority and curriculum.

"Doctrinal matters" **do NOT** include:

a) Steward compensation (governed by COI Policy §6 §4958 procedure);
b) Allocation or disposition of Community assets above any threshold;
c) Arm's-length transactions with any Brand LLC or other commercial counterparty;
d) Adoption, amendment, or enforcement of the Conflict of Interest Policy;
e) Records & Privacy Protocol matters;
f) Adoption or amendment of these Bylaws (which are governed by the supermajority and hard-rule procedures);
g) Any matter within the exclusive authority of the Stewardship Council under §3.02.

In the event of dispute over whether a matter is doctrinal or fiduciary, the Stewardship Council's determination of fiduciary character is final, and the Founding Steward's veto under this §5.07 does not apply to matters so determined.

The Founding Steward veto is exercised in writing within fourteen (14) days of the decision and recorded in the Member meeting minutes. A Member supermajority of three-fourths (3/4) may override a doctrinal veto. The Founding Steward may not exercise this veto on any matter from which the Founding Steward is recused under the Conflict of Interest Policy.

## ARTICLE VI — Conflict of Interest

### §6.01 Conflict of Interest Policy
The full Conflict of Interest Policy is set forth in Constitutional Pack Document #6 and is incorporated into these Bylaws by reference.

### §6.02 Required Disclosures
Every Steward, Stewardship Council member, and Pastoral Council member shall annually disclose to the Stewardship Council Chair:

a) Ownership interest in any Brand LLC or other entity that does business with CORA Nation;
b) Material commercial, contractual, or familial relationships with other Stewards or Council members;
c) Any compensation, consulting, or service relationship with CORA Nation entities;
d) Any other matter the disclosing person reasonably believes might create a conflict of interest.

### §6.03 Recusal
A Steward, Council member, or voting Member shall recuse from any decision in which they (or a related party as defined in the COI Policy) have a material economic interest. The Allocation Platform shall enforce recusal at the protocol layer where technically feasible.

### §6.04 §4958 Rebuttable-Presumption
Any community-provision compensation, Brand-LLC charitable contribution, or other transaction with a related party is subject to the §4958 Rebuttable-Presumption procedure set forth in the Conflict of Interest Policy. The platform shall auto-generate contemporaneous documentation per Treasury v0.10 §Founder compensation requirements.

## ARTICLE VII — Stewardship Council

### §7.01 Composition
The Stewardship Council consists of not fewer than three (3) and not more than nine (9) individuals, recognized as Stewards per the Pastoral Leadership Structure, of whom a majority shall be independent per Article III §3.04.

### §7.02 Term
Stewardship Council members serve staggered three-year terms. Initial appointments are by the Founding Steward subject to Member ratification at the first Annual Meeting; thereafter, the Stewardship Council nominates successors subject to Member ratification per §5.02.

### §7.03 Authority
The Stewardship Council exercises the secular fiduciary authority described in Article III §3.02. The Council acts by majority of those present at a duly noticed meeting at which a quorum (majority of total Council) is present.

### §7.04 Removal
A Stewardship Council member may be removed by Member supermajority per §5.03(c) or, for cause as defined in the Pastoral Leadership Structure, by joint action of the Pastoral Council and a majority of the remaining Stewardship Council members.

### §7.05 No Compensation for Council Service
Stewardship Council members serve without compensation for their Council role. Council members who hold other Stewardship roles (e.g., operating Stewards) may receive community-provision compensation per §6.04 for those separate roles, with recusal per §6.03.

## ARTICLE VIII — Pastoral Council

### §8.01 Composition
The Pastoral Council consists of the Founding Steward and not fewer than two (2) additional individuals recognized by the Founding Steward and ratified by Member supermajority per §5.03.

### §8.02 Authority
The Pastoral Council exercises the spiritual, doctrinal, and pastoral authority described in Article III §3.01.

### §8.03 Doctrinal Amendments
Amendments to Canon proposed by the Pastoral Council are subject to Member supermajority ratification per §5.03(b) but cannot originate from the Membership alone — the Pastoral Council is the canonical originating body.

### §8.04 Pastoral Discipline
The Pastoral Council exercises discipline and restoration of Members per the Admission & Exit Procedures.

## ARTICLE IX — Officers

The Community has the following Officers, each appointed by the Stewardship Council from among its members for one-year terms:

a) **Chair** — presides at Stewardship Council meetings and Member meetings
b) **Treasurer** — maintains the financial records, prepares the annual budget, oversees compliance with the Records & Privacy Protocol financial-records provisions
c) **Secretary** — maintains the Records, prepares meeting notices and minutes, oversees member-roll integrity
d) **Counsel Liaison** — coordinates with external counsel and maintains the IRS Examination Readiness Index

Officers may hold other Stewardship roles. The Founding Steward is not eligible to serve as Treasurer.

## ARTICLE XI — Indemnification

To the maximum extent permitted under the laws of the State of Florida (and any other applicable law to which CORA Nation is subject), CORA Nation shall indemnify any Steward, Council member, Officer, or Member acting in good faith in the conduct of Community affairs against expenses, liabilities, and losses incurred in connection with such service, except for conduct constituting willful misconduct or knowing violation of law.

This Article XI shall be reconfirmed or amended at v1.0 consistent with external counsel's determination of the applicable Florida statutory framework for unincorporated religious associations and any insurance-coverage requirements under Constitutional Pack Document #8 (IRS Examination Readiness Index) activation preconditions.

## ARTICLE XII — Amendment

### §12.01 Standard Bylaw Amendment
Subject to §12.02, these Bylaws may be amended by Member supermajority per §5.03(a) following written proposal noticed not less than thirty (30) days in advance.

### §12.02 Non-Amendable Hard-Rules
The following provisions of these Bylaws and any platform implementing them are **hard-rules** that may NOT be amended by Member vote, Stewardship Council resolution, or any other ordinary process:

a) Article I §1.02 (Florida domicile and governing law);
b) Article I §1.05 (No Profit Motive);
c) Article II §2.03 (Religious Character of Membership);
d) Article II §2.04 (No Transfer of Membership);
e) Article III §3.03 (Non-overlap Principle);
f) Article V §5.05 (Allocation Platform protocol-layer constants, upon and from Member ratification of the Allocation Platform spec);
g) Article V §5.06 (Concentration Limit and one-hop delegation);
h) Article VI §6.03 (Recusal);
i) Article VI §6.04 (§4958 Rebuttable-Presumption);
j) Article XII §12.03 (Founding-Stage Activation Gate);
k) Article XII §12.04 (State Charitable Solicitation Registration Gate);
l) Article XIII §13.01 (Dissolution distribution requirement);
m) This Article XII §12.02 itself.

Modification of any hard-rule requires (i) recommendation by external religious-nonprofit counsel, (ii) Member supermajority of nine-tenths (9/10) of all Members (not merely those voting), and (iii) a separate technical release process for any platform implementing the affected rule.

This restriction exists to prevent the Membership from voting to disable the structural securities, inurement, and religious-character protections on which CORA Nation's legal posture depends.

### §12.03 Founding-Stage Activation Gate

No community-provision compensation to the Founding Steward or any Steward, no allocation of Community resources above the §5.04 threshold, no related-party transaction within the meaning of the Conflict of Interest Policy, and no disposition of Community assets shall be authorized until ALL of the following have been satisfied:

a) **At least three (3) independent Stewardship Council members have been seated**, with documented independence per Article III §3.04 (not Related Parties of the Founding Steward, no Brand LLC ownership stake of the Founding Steward, no close personal or business-partnership relationship with the Founding Steward);

b) Each seated Stewardship Council member has completed initial COI disclosures per Conflict of Interest Policy §4.1;

c) For any compensation determination, the §4958 Rebuttable-Presumption procedure under Conflict of Interest Policy §6 has been completed and documented contemporaneously;

d) External religious-nonprofit counsel has reviewed and signed off on the Constitutional Pack v1.0 per Constitutional Pack README §Activation Sequence.

This Gate is a hard-rule under §12.02 and may not be relaxed or waived by Member vote, Stewardship Council resolution, or Founding Steward action. The Gate exists to prevent the founding-stage gap in Bifurcated Authority (where the Founding Steward holds both spiritual and de facto fiduciary authority until the Council is constituted) from translating into private inurement, even temporarily.

### §12.04 State Charitable Solicitation Registration Gate

CORA Nation shall not solicit charitable contributions, donations, or membership-fee equivalents from residents of any state requiring registration of charitable organizations under that state's Solicitation of Contributions Act (or equivalent) until:

a) External counsel has completed a 50-state survey identifying which states require registration and which exemptions apply to CORA Nation as a §508(c)(1)(A) religious organization (per Brand Tok v0.4 Addendum C counsel deliverable §2);

b) Registration has been completed in each state where required (or exemption has been confirmed and documented).

In Florida, where CORA Nation is domiciled, the religious-organization exemption under F.S. 496.403 applies. The Counsel Liaison shall maintain the registration / exemption status for each state in which CORA Nation has Members or solicits contributions.

This Gate is a hard-rule under §12.02. It is not intended to prevent religious-community-internal flows (Member contributions consistent with religious covenant) but rather to prevent unregistered public solicitation that would create state-level enforcement exposure.

## ARTICLE XIII — Dissolution

### §13.01 Dissolution Distribution
Upon dissolution of CORA Nation, all assets remaining after payment of debts and obligations shall be distributed exclusively for religious or charitable purposes within the meaning of Internal Revenue Code §501(c)(3), to one or more organizations qualified under §501(c)(3) and engaged in activities consonant with the religious purpose of CORA Nation, as determined by the Stewardship Council with the consent of the Pastoral Council. No assets shall be distributed to Members, Stewards, Officers, or any private party.

### §13.02 Dissolution Procedure
Dissolution requires (i) recommendation by the Founding Steward and the Pastoral Council, (ii) Member supermajority of three-fourths (3/4) of all Members (not merely those voting), and (iii) the dissolution plan must be reviewed by counsel before the dissolution vote.

## ARTICLE XIV — Adoption and Ratification

### §14.01 Initial Adoption
These Bylaws are adopted by the Founding Steward effective on the date of signing below, and shall govern CORA Nation in interim form until ratification by the Membership per §14.02.

**§14.01(a) Maximum Interim Period.** The interim period shall not exceed one hundred eighty (180) days from the date of signing. The Founding Steward shall use best efforts to seat at least three (3) independent Stewardship Council members within sixty (60) days of signing and to convene the first Annual Meeting within one hundred fifty (150) days of signing.

**§14.01(b) Interim-Period Limitations.** During the interim period, and at any time before the Founding-Stage Activation Gate (§12.03) is satisfied:

a) No community-provision compensation may be authorized;
b) No allocation above the §5.04 threshold may be authorized;
c) No related-party transaction may be authorized;
d) No disposition of Community assets may be authorized;
e) No PMA Membership Agreements may be signed by new Members (Inquirer-stage participation continues; Apprentice-stage formal admission begins only after Gate is satisfied).

The Founding Steward may, during the interim period, conduct ceremonial, doctrinal, pastoral, and Inquirer-stage activity; engage external counsel; assemble candidate Stewardship Council members; and prepare the Constitutional Pack v1.0 for ratification.

If the interim period exceeds 180 days without the Founding-Stage Activation Gate being satisfied, the Founding Steward shall report the status to the Membership (Inquirers and any Apprentices admitted before the Gate's adoption) and shall seek extension by written commitment to a revised timeline. Any extension beyond 180 days shall be limited to circumstances genuinely beyond the Founding Steward's control (such as delayed counsel engagement) and shall be documented in writing.

### §14.02 Member Ratification
Following counsel review of the Constitutional Pack v1.0, these Bylaws shall be presented to the Membership for ratification at the first Annual Meeting following counsel sign-off. Ratification requires Member supermajority per §5.03(a). Until ratification, these Bylaws govern as the Founding Steward's interim charter.

### §14.03 Conflict with Canon
In the event of conflict between these Bylaws and the Canon, the Canon governs on doctrinal matters and these Bylaws govern on governance and fiduciary matters. The Pastoral Council and the Stewardship Council jointly resolve conflicts at the doctrinal/fiduciary boundary.
