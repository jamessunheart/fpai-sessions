## 1. Purpose

The integrity of CORA Nation's religious-community status under IRC §508(c)(1)(A) — and the legal defensibility of every operational structure built on top of it (Brand LLC tier, Coherent Credit, Allocation Platform) — depends on **structural prevention of private inurement and self-dealing**. This Policy operationalizes that prevention.

Per Treasury v0.10 §Governance Firewall:

> *"Private inurement is the number one cause of intentional religious community legal destruction... Architectural separation between spiritual authority and secular fiduciary control is the structural defense."*

This Policy establishes:

a) Required disclosures by persons in fiduciary, pastoral, or operational roles;
b) Recusal procedures, enforced structurally where technically feasible;
c) The §4958 Rebuttable-Presumption procedure for compensation and benefit transactions;
d) Independent review requirements;
e) Records retention for examination readiness.

---

## 2. Covered Persons

This Policy applies to all of the following ("**Covered Persons**"):

a) The Founding Steward;
b) Members of the Pastoral Council;
c) Members of the Stewardship Council;
d) Stewards (Operating, Pastoral, Fiduciary, or Specialty) per Pastoral Leadership Structure §4.2;
e) Officers per Bylaws Article IX;
f) Any Member receiving community-provision compensation;
g) Any Member exercising allocation authority above the Bylaws §5.04 threshold;
h) Any Member whose contracted services to CORA Nation exceed USD $5,000 per year.

---

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Conflict of Interest
A "**Conflict of Interest**" exists when a Covered Person has, or would receive, an actual or potential direct or indirect economic, family, or personal-relationship benefit from a CORA Nation decision, transaction, or allocation, such that the Covered Person's judgment on the matter would or could reasonably be perceived to be influenced.

### 3.2 Related Party
A "**Related Party**" to a Covered Person is:

a) The Covered Person's spouse, domestic partner, parent, sibling, child, or grandchild;
b) An entity (including a Brand LLC) in which the Covered Person or any of the foregoing has a 5%-or-greater ownership interest, a directorship or officer role, or a material contractual relationship;
c) Any other person with whom the Covered Person has a sustained personal, romantic, or financial relationship of material significance.

### 3.3 Material Benefit
A "**Material Benefit**" is any economic, in-kind, or service benefit valued at USD $1,000 or more in a single transaction or USD $5,000 or more in aggregate per fiscal year, or any non-pecuniary benefit (such as access, status, or influence) that a reasonable person would consider influential on judgment.

### 3.4 Excess Benefit Transaction
"**Excess Benefit Transaction**" has the meaning given in IRC §4958: any transaction in which an economic benefit is provided directly or indirectly to or for the use of a disqualified person, the value of which exceeds the value of consideration received by the organization.

## 4. Required Disclosures

### 4.1 Annual Disclosure
Each Covered Person shall, within thirty (30) days of recognition as a Covered Person and annually thereafter at a date set by the Stewardship Council, disclose in writing to the Stewardship Council Chair (with copy to the Counsel Liaison) all of the following:

a) Ownership interests of 5% or more in any Brand LLC or other for-profit or non-profit entity that has, or is reasonably anticipated to have, a transactional or contractual relationship with CORA Nation;

b) Directorships, officer roles, or material contractual relationships with entities described in (a);

c) Related Parties (per §3.2) who have interests of the type described in (a) or (b);

d) Personal, romantic, or business-partnership relationships with other Covered Persons that could create perceived or actual conflicts;

e) Any compensation, consulting, royalty, or service arrangement with CORA Nation, its Brand LLCs, or the Sunheart Private Trust;

f) Any pending litigation, government inquiry, or regulatory action involving the Covered Person that could reasonably affect the Covered Person's service to CORA Nation;

g) Any other matter the Covered Person reasonably believes might create an actual or perceived Conflict of Interest.

### 4.2 Event-Driven Disclosure
A Covered Person shall update the disclosure within fourteen (14) days of any material change to disclosable matters.

### 4.3 Acknowledgment of Policy
Each Covered Person shall annually acknowledge in writing that the Covered Person has read this Policy, understands its requirements, and will comply.

### 4.4 Records of Disclosure
Disclosures are maintained by the Counsel Liaison per the Records & Privacy Protocol with access limited to: the Stewardship Council, the Pastoral Council, external counsel, and the disclosing Covered Person. Aggregated, anonymized statistics may be reported to the Membership at the Annual Meeting.

## 5. Recusal

### 5.1 Mandatory Recusal
A Covered Person shall recuse from any CORA Nation decision, vote, or deliberation in which the Covered Person or a Related Party would receive a Material Benefit, or in which the Covered Person's judgment could reasonably be perceived as influenced by an interest disclosed under §4.1.

### 5.2 Recusal Process
Recusal includes:

a) Disclosing the conflict at the start of the relevant deliberation;
b) Withdrawing from the discussion before substantive deliberation begins (physically leaving the room or electronic session);
c) Not voting on the matter;
d) The fact of recusal being recorded in meeting minutes.

### 5.3 Structural Enforcement
Where the decision is made through the Allocation Platform (when implemented) or other electronic systems, recusal shall be enforced at the protocol layer:

a) The conflict matrix shall be queried before a Covered Person can vote on or receive a flow from any matter implicating that Covered Person;
b) Self-conflicted attempts shall return a hard 403 (forbidden) and shall be logged;
c) The conflict matrix is maintained from the disclosures in §4 and is updated within forty-eight (48) hours of any event-driven disclosure;
d) Manual override of structural recusal is not permitted.

### 5.4 Founding Steward Recusal
The Founding Steward shall recuse from:

a) Any decision regarding the Founding Steward's own community-provision compensation;
b) Any decision regarding a Brand LLC in which the Founding Steward holds an ownership interest;
c) Any decision regarding compensation, benefits, or allocations to Related Parties of the Founding Steward;
d) Any other matter meeting §5.1 grounds.

The Founding Steward's doctrinal veto per Bylaws §5.07 may not be exercised on matters from which the Founding Steward is recused.

### 5.5 Recusal Does Not Suspend Membership
A recused Covered Person retains all other Member rights and obligations and is not in a disciplinary posture by virtue of recusal alone.

## 6. §4958 Rebuttable-Presumption Procedure

This procedure applies to any **Compensation** or **Benefit Transaction** between CORA Nation and a Covered Person or Related Party, including:

a) Community-provision compensation to the Founding Steward, Pastoral Council members, Stewardship Council members, or Stewards;
b) Charitable contributions from a Brand LLC to CORA Nation where the Brand LLC is operated by a Covered Person or Related Party;
c) Service contracts between CORA Nation and a Covered Person or entity controlled by a Covered Person or Related Party;
d) Asset transfers between CORA Nation, the Sunheart Private Trust, and any Covered Person or entity controlled by a Covered Person or Related Party;
e) Any other transaction implicating Excess Benefit Transaction analysis.

### 6.1 Comparable-Compensation Data

For any compensation determination, the Counsel Liaison or designee shall obtain comparable-compensation data that satisfies the "appropriate data as to comparability" standard of Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6(c)(2), specifically:

**§6.1(a) Minimum sources.** Comparable-compensation data shall be obtained from at least three (3) of the following sources, with at least one of categories (i) and (ii) required: (i) BLS clergy / nonprofit-executive data; (ii) peer religious-organization surveys (e.g., from religious-nonprofit associations, denominational benchmarking, or third-party religious-org compensation studies); (iii) IRS Form 990 / Form 990-T data of comparable religious organizations or religious-nonprofits voluntarily disclosing comparable data; (iv) published academic or industry compensation studies covering religious-nonprofit compensation; (v) counsel-provided benchmarks from comparable engagements.

**§6.1(b) Comparability standard.** Each comparable organization used in the analysis shall satisfy all of the following:

i) **Same religious-community type** — intentional religious community, retreat-oriented religious organization, or religious-community-operating-physical-substrate (not generic 501(c)(3) charity);

ii) **Comparable size band** — organizational membership within an order-of-magnitude range (e.g., 10-100 members compared to 10-100 members; 100-1,000 to 100-1,000), and organizational revenue within a similar band;

iii) **Comparable geographic region** — U.S. religious-community comparable for U.S. compensation; Costa Rica or Latin American religious-community comparable for CR-located compensation; international comparable acceptable with documented adjustment for cost-of-living differential;

iv) **Comparable role scope** — the comparable role shall have similar duties, time commitment, and pastoral/operational responsibility to the role being benchmarked.

**§6.1(c) Documentation per data point.** For each data point used in the analysis, the Memorandum shall document: (i) source identification with enough specificity that an independent reviewer could retrieve the underlying data (e.g., specific publication name, date, page; specific Form 990 organization name, year, line item); (ii) the comparable organization's size, role scope, and region; (iii) any adjustments made (and the basis for those adjustments).

**§6.1(d) Counsel review.** The Comparable-Compensation Memorandum shall be reviewed by external religious-nonprofit counsel before first use (i.e., before the first §4958 procedure is run for any Steward) and annually thereafter. Counsel's review confirms the Memorandum meets the comparability standard under Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6 and identifies any deficiencies before reliance.

**§6.1(e) Comparable-Compensation Memorandum.** The data shall be assembled into a written **Comparable-Compensation Memorandum** retained per the Records & Privacy Protocol §2.7.

**§6.1(f) Platform registry supplements, does not replace, review.** The Allocation Platform (when implemented) shall maintain a structured comparable-compensation registry capturing all fields required by §6.1(b) and §6.1(c), and shall auto-generate the Memorandum from the registry. Auto-generation **supplements but does not replace** independent-Council review of actual data per §6.2 — the Council must read and deliberate on the substance, not merely accept the auto-generated artifact.

### 6.2 Independent Approval
The Compensation or Benefit Transaction shall be approved by **independent Stewardship Council members**, defined as Stewardship Council members who:

a) Are not the Covered Person being compensated;
b) Are not Related Parties of the Covered Person being compensated;
c) Do not have any direct or indirect economic interest in the transaction;
d) Constitute at least a majority of the approving body.

For Founding Steward compensation, all approving Stewardship Council members shall be independent of the Founding Steward (not Related Parties, not in business partnership, not in close personal relationship).

The independent Stewardship Council members shall:

a) Review the Comparable-Compensation Memorandum;
b) Deliberate the determination in a meeting with quorum;
c) Approve the determination by majority vote;
d) Record the determination, basis, and supporting data in meeting minutes.

### 6.3 Contemporaneous Documentation
The determination, basis, comparable data, and approving Council members shall be documented in writing **at the time of approval** (not after) per Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6. The documentation shall include:

a) The names of approving Council members and confirmation of their independence;
b) The Comparable-Compensation Memorandum;
c) The approved compensation amount, terms, and effective date;
d) The basis for the determination (why the approved amount is reasonable);
e) Date of approval;
f) Signatures or electronic acknowledgments of approving Council members.

### 6.4 Auto-Generation by Allocation Platform
When the Allocation Platform is operational, the §4958 documentation shall be auto-generated from platform data on a weekly cron, and shall be presented to the Counsel Liaison for review and counter-signature.

Auto-generation does **not** replace the requirement of independent-Council approval per §6.2; auto-generation produces the documentation of approval that was independently given.

### 6.5 Pre-Activation Procedure
For any Steward whose community-provision compensation is to commence, the §4958 procedure shall be completed and documented **before** the first community-provision flows. The procedure is a **hard activation precondition** under Bylaws §12.02(i) and is further gated by Bylaws §12.03 **Founding-Stage Activation Gate**:

a) At least three (3) independent Stewardship Council members must be seated per Bylaws Article III §3.04;
b) Initial COI disclosures per §4.1 of this Policy must be on file from each seated Council member;
c) The §6.1 Comparable-Compensation Memorandum must have been counsel-reviewed per §6.1(d);
d) The §6.2 independent-Council approval must have occurred at a duly noticed Council meeting with quorum;
e) The §6.3 contemporaneous documentation must be complete before the first flow.

The Founding-Stage Activation Gate is a Bylaws-level hard-rule that cannot be relaxed by Council resolution, Member vote, or Founding Steward action.

### 6.6 Periodic Review
Each compensation arrangement shall be reviewed under this §6 procedure at least annually, or upon any material change in scope of role.

## 7. Independent Review

### 7.1 External Counsel
For transactions involving the Founding Steward, the Sunheart Private Trust, or transactions exceeding USD $50,000 per fiscal year, the Stewardship Council shall consult with external religious-nonprofit counsel before approval.

### 7.2 Brand LLC Charitable Contributions
Charitable contributions from a Brand LLC to CORA Nation shall be:

a) Initiated by the Brand LLC's own decision-making (not by CORA Nation request);
b) Documented as voluntary, with no quid pro quo per Brand Tok v0.4 Addendum B.2;
c) Reviewed by the Counsel Liaison and the independent Stewardship Council members for arm's-length compliance;
d) Disclosed in the Annual Report to the Membership.

### 7.3 Brand LLC Service Engagements
If CORA Nation engages a Brand LLC for services (e.g., catering a retreat, providing communications platform), the engagement shall be:

a) Subject to recusal of any Steward owning the Brand LLC;
b) Priced at arm's-length market rate;
c) Documented per the Records & Privacy Protocol;
d) Reviewed if the cumulative annual engagement exceeds USD $25,000.

---

## 8. Brand LLC Tier Separation

Per Brand Tok v0.4 Addendum B.1, each Brand LLC is independent of CORA Nation. This Policy enforces that separation by:

a) Prohibiting CORA Nation board seat, veto, or operational direction over Brand LLCs;
b) Prohibiting CORA Nation equity, debt, profit-share, or revenue-share in Brand LLCs;
c) Requiring Steward recusal on any CORA Nation matter affecting a Brand LLC the Steward owns or operates;
d) Requiring binary-only (not balance-based) membership verification for member-rate discounts (per Brand Tok v0.4 Refinements: §Brand LLC membership verification).

## 9. Member Rights and Education

### 9.1 Member Awareness
All Members shall be informed of the existence and substance of this Policy at the time of PMA Membership Agreement signing. Apprentices read this Policy as part of Apprentice practices per Admission & Exit Procedures §4.3(c).

### 9.2 Member Complaint
Any Member may report a suspected Conflict of Interest violation in writing to the Counsel Liaison, with copy to the Pastoral Council Chair. The complaint shall be reviewed and resolved per §10.

### 9.3 No Retaliation
No Covered Person shall retaliate against a Member who in good faith reports a suspected violation. Retaliation is itself a violation of this Policy.

---

## 10. Violations

### 10.1 Investigation
A suspected violation shall be investigated by the Counsel Liaison and the independent Stewardship Council members. The investigation shall:

a) Notify the Covered Person whose conduct is at issue;
b) Provide the Covered Person opportunity to respond;
c) Review records and disclosures;
d) Document findings in writing.

### 10.2 Remedy
If a violation is found, remedies may include:

a) Unwinding the offending transaction (return of excess benefit by Covered Person);
b) Imposition of §4958 first-tier or second-tier excise tax (a tax owed by the Covered Person to the IRS — CORA Nation does not pay it);
c) Removal from Steward role or Council position;
d) Membership discipline per Pastoral Leadership Structure §6;
e) Filing of IRS Form 4720 if required by law;
f) Public disclosure to the Membership at the Annual Meeting (anonymized as appropriate).

### 10.3 External Counsel Consultation
For any violation involving Excess Benefit Transaction characterization or sums exceeding USD $10,000, external counsel shall be consulted before remedy is finalized.

## 11. Records

All records under this Policy are maintained per the Records & Privacy Protocol with:

a) Indefinite retention;
b) Access limited to the Stewardship Council, the Pastoral Council, the Counsel Liaison, external counsel, and (for one's own record) the disclosing Covered Person;
c) Examiner-exportable on formal request;
d) Annual aggregate reporting to Membership.

## 12. Allocation Platform Integration Requirements

The Allocation Platform spec (downstream of this Constitutional Pack) shall implement at minimum the following structural enforcement of this Policy:

a) Maintained conflict matrix from disclosures in §4;
b) Pre-vote conflict check that returns 403 (forbidden) on self-conflicted vote attempts;
c) Pre-flow conflict check that returns 403 on flow attempts where the recipient is Self-conflicted with a Covered Person on the approving side;
d) Weekly auto-generation of §4958 Comparable-Compensation Memoranda from registry data;
e) Audit log of all conflict-check results (including allowed and denied attempts);
f) No manual override path.

These platform requirements are hard-rules under Bylaws §12.02(e) and may not be amended by Member vote.
