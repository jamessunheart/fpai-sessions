# COUNSEL Q1 — Does 508(c)(1)(A) Cover Soultime Bank Member-to-Member Exchange?

**Status:** Research synthesis from a non-attorney AI legal-critic substitute. NOT legal advice. Use this to scope the questions for a licensed attorney's sanity-pass before any launch.
**Date:** 2026-05-19
**Author:** AI research synthesis standing in for The Counsel
**Question framed:** Does CORA Nation's 508(c)(1)(A) status legally cover internal member-to-member economic exchange of soul-time credits as church scrip, with what specific structural and operational requirements, and at what scale does this protection break down?

---

## 1. Direct answer (≤200 words)

**Conditional yes — but 508(c)(1)(A) alone is the WRONG anchor.** 508(c)(1)(A) only excuses the church from filing Form 1023; it does NOT confer any substantive economic-exchange privilege beyond ordinary 501(c)(3) rules. A 501(c)(3) church that runs member-to-member economic exchange faces three immediate doctrines: **private benefit** (the operational test), **private inurement** (absolute bar), and **UBIT** on commercial activity.

**The actually-protective anchor is IRC §501(d)** — the apostolic-association exemption for religious communities with a **common treasury** that engages in business for common benefit (Hutterite/Bruderhof precedent, 90+ years of case law). §501(d) is exactly designed for what Soultime Bank v0.1 wants to do. It comes with one heavy cost: members report **pro rata share of community income as a dividend on personal returns**, even if not distributed.

**Operative path:** v0.1 launches as an internal benefactor-recognition ledger inside CORA Nation (508(c)(1)(A) shell) **with §501(d) structural elements pre-built so the elevation is mechanical when scale demands it.** SC is non-transferable, non-redeemable for fiat, gift/benefactor-coded. Three founding-cohort attorney sanity passes ($2–5k each) before M2 transactions, not after.

## 2. Legal basis citations

### 2.1 IRC §508(c)(1)(A) — what it actually does

- **Text:** "Mandatory exceptions [to §508(a) registration]. Subsection (a) shall not apply to — (A) churches, their integrated auxiliaries, and conventions or associations of churches."
- **Effect:** Excuses Form 1023 application. **Nothing more.** The church is still bound by 501(c)(3) operational rules — exclusively religious purpose, no private inurement, limited private benefit, UBIT on unrelated commercial income.
- **Common myth (debunked by IRS, StartChurch, Church Law Center, NCLL):** "508 free church" status does NOT exempt the church from 501(c)(3) substantive rules. It is a filing convenience only.
- **Citation:** IRC §508(c)(1)(A); Treas. Reg. §1.508-1; IRS Pub 1828 "Tax Guide for Churches and Religious Organizations."

### 2.2 IRC §501(d) — the actual protective anchor

- **Text:** "Religious or apostolic associations or corporations, if such associations or corporations have a common treasury or community treasury, even if such associations or corporations engage in business for the common benefit of the members, but only if the members thereof include (at the time of filing their returns) in their gross income their entire pro rata shares… of the taxable income of the association or corporation for such year."
- **Treas. Reg. §1.501(d)-1** elaborates four requirements:
  1. Religious or apostolic association/corporation
  2. Common treasury or community treasury
  3. May engage in business for common benefit of members
  4. Members include entire pro rata share of organization's net taxable income as a dividend on personal return (whether distributed or not)
- **Filing:** Form 1065 (treated like a partnership for accounting, exempt itself from federal income tax)
- **Precedent:** Hutterite, Bruderhof, Amish communal corporations have used this for ~90 years. *Stahl Hutterian Brethren v. Commissioner* (9th Cir.) confirmed corporate-style treatment available.
- **Citation:** IRC §501(d); Treas. Reg. §1.501(d)-1; Rev. Rul. 78-100; IRS Pub 5627 "Religious and Apostolic Associations."

### 2.3 IRC §4958 — intermediate sanctions / excess benefit

- Applies to 501(c)(3) and 501(c)(4) — explicitly does NOT apply to 501(d) per the statute text.
- 25% excise on disqualified person; 200% if uncorrected; 10% (capped at $20k) on participating manager.
- **Brand Tok v0.4 already requires §4958 rebuttable presumption procedure** (comparable data + independent approval + contemporaneous docs) before any Steward compensation. Soultime Bank must inherit this.
- **Citation:** IRC §4958; Treas. Reg. §53.4958-1 through -6.

### 2.4 IRC §6045(c) and Treas. Reg. §1.6045-1(a)(4) — barter exchange reporting

- A "barter exchange" = "any person with members or clients that contract either with each other or with such person to jointly trade or barter property or services."
- An organized barter exchange must issue Form 1099-B for FMV exchanged per member per year.
- **Two safe harbors:** (i) "informal exchanges of similar services on a noncommercial basis" are exempt (Treas. Reg. §1.6045-1(a)(4) preamble); (ii) exchanges of FMV under $1.00 are de minimis.
- **The risk:** A formalized ledger with AI-computed FMV (1 SC = $100 reference rate) looks formal enough that the "informal noncommercial" safe harbor may not hold without specific structural language.
- **Citation:** IRC §6045; Treas. Reg. §1.6045-1; IRS Topic No. 420 (Bartering Income); Pub 525.

### 2.5 IRC §1402(g) — vow of poverty exemption (limited applicability)

- Members of religious orders who have taken a vow of poverty are exempt from SE tax on ministerial services for the order.
- **NOT a tax-avoidance vehicle.** IRS aggressively prosecutes sham "vow of poverty" arrangements where the member retains beneficial use of assets.
- Could apply to a subset of founding villagers who genuinely embrace common-treasury life — but this is a heavy structural commitment, not a paperwork move.

### 2.6 Howey test — SEC v. W.J. Howey Co., 328 U.S. 293 (1946)

Investment contract = (i) investment of money (ii) in a common enterprise (iii) with reasonable expectation of profit (iv) derived from efforts of others.

- **Soultime credit v0.1 architecture survives Howey IF:** no transferability, no secondary market, no fiat redemption, no marketed profit expectation, member earns SC by their own labor (not "from others' efforts").
- **Equity tier / patronage dividend = re-opens Howey.** If founding villagers are promised a yield share derived from Bank's reserve investments, that's a passive investor relationship with "efforts of others" — classic security.
- **Recommended:** Defer equity tier to v0.3 cooperative or §501(d) pro-rata-dividend structure. Do not promise yields denominated against external investment returns in v0.1.

### 2.7 FinCEN — money transmitter exemption

- 31 CFR §1010.100(ff)(5)(ii): MSB excludes value not "redeemable for currency."
- FinCEN Guidance FIN-2019-G001: closed-loop value redeemable only for goods/services from issuer/affiliates is NOT money transmission.
- **v0.1 architecture safe IF:** no SC → fiat redemption offered, no SC → SC transfer to outside members, all redemption is for in-network goods/services only.
- Mass. Div. of Banks (Op. Letter 2014) treated non-convertible local currency as outside money transmission scope.

### 2.8 State money transmitter law — closed-loop exemption

- CSBS Model Money Transmission Modernization Act (MTMA, adopted 2024+) exempts closed-loop stored value: "redeemable by the issuer only for goods or services provided by the issuer, its affiliate or a franchisee."
- **Wyoming, Texas, Florida, NM, CA** all have variants of this exemption.
- **Trigger that breaks the exemption:** allowing redemption for cash in any state with >$X threshold member presence.

### 2.9 IRS hostility precedent — TimeBanks USA

- IRS denied 501(c)(3) exempt status to a time-banking community network (Tax Notes coverage; consistent with multiple denial letter rulings). Reasoning: "operate as cooperative organizations for the mutual benefit of members, serving private interests rather than public purpose."
- **This is the most direct adverse precedent.** A pure member-to-member exchange system, even framed in altruistic language, fails the public-benefit test under 501(c)(3) when scrutinized by IRS.
- **Counter-strategy:** Don't rely on 501(c)(3) framing for the exchange function. Frame the exchange as the **internal economic life of a religious community** (§501(d) territory), distinct from the church's outward charitable purpose.

## 3. Strongest precedents for confidence

1. **Hutterite/Bruderhof §501(d) communal economic life — 90+ years, billions in revenue, never lost exemption.** Direct analogy to Camp Zen common-treasury structure. This is the single strongest precedent.
2. **Stahl Hutterian Brethren v. Commissioner** — 9th Cir. confirmed corporate-style treatment of communal religious organizations.
3. **Rev. Rul. 78-100** — affirms §501(d) qualification standards; specifically permits "engage in business for common benefit of members."
4. **Ithaca Hours / Bristol Pound / WIR Bank** — local community currencies operate legally in US/EU when designed within FinCEN closed-loop framing; the IRS only takes interest at transaction-FMV level for income tax (not currency-issuance level).
5. **Closed-loop scrip exemption under CSBS MTMA** — explicit state-level carve-out for member-only redemption value.
6. **IRS Pub 1828 (Tax Guide for Churches)** — explicitly permits "intra-church" economic activities not subject to UBIT when restricted to members for religious purposes.

## 4. Strongest concerns / failure modes

### 4.1 The IRS time-bank denial is the wall

The IRS has already ruled that pure mutual-benefit member exchange networks fail 501(c)(3). Soultime Bank, framed as a "bank" doing "member-to-member economic exchange," walks directly into this precedent. **The framing must be: this is the internal economic substrate of a religious community (§501(d) territory), not a charitable program (§501(c)(3) territory).** Get this wrong and the entire CORA Nation exempt status is at risk on audit.

### 4.2 Private inurement — absolute bar

If founding villagers (insiders / disqualified persons) receive SC denominated at FMV that exceeds the religious-service value of their contribution, that's private inurement → automatic 501(c)(3) revocation. AI-computed FMV multipliers (specialization × scarcity × demand-fit × member-tier) trend toward exactly this risk.

**Mitigation:** v0.1 settlements explicitly framed as **religious-community benefactor recognition** (not market-FMV compensation). Pricing engine outputs "soul-time recognition units," not "fair market value." Document this in CORA Nation governing docs.

### 4.3 Barter exchange recharacterization — 1099-B trigger

A formal ledger + AI-computed FMV + $100/SC reference rate looks like a barter exchange to the IRS. If recharacterized:
- Form 1099-B required per member per year on aggregate FMV exchanged
- Each member owes income tax on FMV received (Schedule 1 or Schedule C)
- Member UX destroyed; tax surprise at scale

**Mitigation:** §501(d) structure converts this entirely — members report pro rata share of community taxable income as a dividend, regardless of internal flows. Or: keep transactions strictly under noncommercial/informal-exchange safe harbor (no FMV pricing language in v0.1 governing docs).

### 4.4 UBIT exposure

If the Bank invests reserve in yield-producing fiat assets (Pendle PT-sUSDe, JitoSOL) to pay SC yields, that investment income may be UBIT if treated as "regularly carried on trade or business." Investment income is generally NOT UBIT under §512(b)(1) — but **debt-financed income** (§514) and certain partnership/operating-business income IS. JitoSOL staking is novel; counsel needs to opine.

**Mitigation:** Keep yield strategy in passive investment buckets clearly under §512(b)(1) protection (interest, dividends, capital gains). Avoid leveraged or operating-business income at v0.1.

### 4.5 "AI-substrate-as-member" novelty

There is **no precedent** for an AI agent holding a Bank account that earns SC redeemable for fiat (model API costs). This will read to a regulator as:
- Circular money laundering (Bank pays itself)
- Inurement to a service-provider (the Bank's compute vendor) at non-arm's-length rates
- Possible novel "computer as taxpayer" question

**Mitigation:** Structure as: Anthropic/OpenAI/etc. are the legal compute service providers. CORA Nation pays them in USD at posted (arms-length) rates. "Ember earns SC" is **internal accounting fiction** for substrate-budget governance, NOT a legal claim that AI holds property. Document this distinction in writing.

### 4.6 Costa Rica jurisdictional layer

Per `BRAND_TOKENIZATION_ARCHITECTURE_v0.4_CONVERGED.md`, Costa Rica entity (S.A. or S.R.L.) is a blocking precondition for Camp Zen physical operations. Soultime Bank inherits this. If any SC redemption happens physically at Camp Zen CR, CR tax authorities (Hacienda) and possibly SUGEF have a claim. CR has no §501(d) equivalent — the religious-community-economic-life cover is US-only.

## 5. Recommended structural guardrails for v0.1 (8-member cohort)

### 5.1 Documents to add to CORA Nation governing structure

1. **Soultime Recognition Ledger Charter** (NOT "Soultime Bank Charter") — internal policy doc adopted by CORA Nation board declaring:
   - SC is a benefactor-recognition unit, not currency, not money, not a security
   - SC is non-transferable to non-members
   - SC is non-redeemable for fiat
   - SC tracks soul-time contribution to the religious community
   - Religious purpose: "Soul-time maximization as ministerial practice within CORA Nation's apostolic mission"
2. **§501(d) Readiness Addendum** to CORA Nation Bylaws — structural language allowing future elevation to §501(d) common-treasury status without full re-charter. Includes:
   - Common treasury provisions (latent, dormant until activated)
   - Pro rata income reporting protocol (template for future activation)
   - Member commitment language consistent with apostolic-association standards
3. **Member Onboarding Affirmation** — each founding cohort member signs:
   - I am a member of CORA Nation, a 508(c)(1)(A) religious community
   - SC I receive recognizes my ministerial contribution to the community
   - SC carries no economic redemption right against the church
   - I will report any taxable income I receive as required by my own tax counsel
   - I am not making an investment with expectation of profit from the efforts of others
4. **§4958 Rebuttable Presumption Procedure** — already mandated by Brand Tok v0.4. Apply to any SC issuance to disqualified persons (board, founding villagers, James) exceeding standard recognition amounts. Three-element protocol: comparable data + independent approval (non-insider board member) + contemporaneous documentation.
5. **Records Retention Policy** — 7 years minimum. PULSE Records, SC ledger entries, member affirmations, board minutes approving issuance policy, §4958 documentation.

### 5.2 Operational guardrails

- **No FMV language in public-facing docs.** Internal AI ledger may use "$100 reference rate" for engineering convenience, but governing docs say "soul-time recognition unit, non-monetary."
- **No fiat conversion offered to members in v0.1.** Treasury → SC ingress allowed (joining gift); SC → fiat egress NOT allowed.
- **No SC accrual to AI agent that converts to fiat at arm's-length below market rate.** Compute costs paid in USD directly to vendors. AI-substrate SC ledger is internal substrate-budget bookkeeping only.
- **No marketing materials describing yield, return, or profit.** Yield is "expanded ministerial capacity," paid in SC redeemable only for community services.
- **No lending product in v0.1.** Defer to v0.2 minimum.
- **No equity tier in v0.1.** Defer patronage dividends to v0.3 cooperative or §501(d) activation.
- **Reporting cadence:** Quarterly board review of SC issuance, ledger reconciliation, reserve coverage. Annual review of §4958 documentation by independent member.
- **Dispute protocol:** AI mediation first, then 3-member board panel (1 must be non-disqualified-person). Final appeal to Counsel.

### 5.3 What NOT to do (regulatory red flags)

- **Don't call it a "Bank" externally** until §501(d) activation + state charter analysis.
- **Don't allow non-CORA-members to hold SC** under any circumstance until charter.
- **Don't permit SC → SC transfers to outside parties** (cross-village v0.3 needs its own counsel pass).
- **Don't promise yields denominated in fiat.**
- **Don't accept member deposits in fiat at >$10k aggregate per member per year** without KYC/AML consistent with CORA Nation AML/KYC Policy v0.1.
- **Don't publish "Soultime Bank" press / social content** until counsel sign-off — the word "Bank" alone is enough to draw state banking regulator attention. ICBA and CFPB monitor "bank" usage closely.

## 6. Scale threshold table — when each protection breaks

| Protection | Safe range | Caution zone | Likely breaks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **508(c)(1)(A) church filing exemption** | Any size, as long as religious-org test met | — | Never breaks on size alone; breaks on operational test |
| **501(c)(3) operational test (private benefit)** | 8–25 members, hand-vetted, religious purpose dominant | 25–100 members, mixed motives | >100 members or any non-religious-purpose marketing — IRS time-bank precedent triggers |
| **Private inurement (absolute)** | Insiders receive SC ≤ value of religious-service contribution, documented | Any quantitative mismatch trending insider-favorable | Single excess-benefit transaction = automatic revocation risk |
| **§501(d) common-treasury (if activated)** | 8–10,000+ members; Hutterite colonies operate at 100+ each, many colonies aggregate to 50,000+ | — | Breaks when "common treasury" is not actually common (members retain separate assets, paychecks, etc.) — substance over form |
| **FinCEN closed-loop (federal MSB)** | Any size as long as no fiat redemption + no transfer to non-members | Mixed redemption (some in-network, some fiat) | Any fiat redemption to non-members → MSB registration required immediately |
| **State money transmitter (CSBS MTMA closed-loop)** | Any size, closed-loop only | Mixed redemption in any state | Same trigger as FinCEN; per-state analysis needed |
| **Barter exchange / 1099-B** | <$1 per transaction OR informal-noncommercial OR §501(d) structure | Formal ledger + FMV pricing language | Aggregate FMV exchanged per member per year >$600 + organized exchange structure = 1099-B duty |
| **UBIT** | Investment income passive (§512(b)(1)) + member services for religious purpose | Active business operations regularly carried on | >$1k/yr unrelated trade/business income from non-members → Form 990-T |
| **Howey / securities** | No transferability, no fiat redemption, no marketed yield, no equity tier | Patronage dividend tied to investment returns | Equity tier + yield denominated in fiat + secondary market = SEC enforcement |
| **CR Hacienda / SUGEF (if CR operations)** | <$0 in CR (US-only operations) | Any physical SC redemption in CR | Camp Zen CR retreat accepts SC as payment → CR tax authority claims; SUGEF has cooperativa-charter regime |

**The single hardest threshold:** Once any member can redeem SC for fiat at a posted rate, virtually every protection above tightens or breaks. Stay strictly closed-loop in v0.1.

**Practical scale frame for v0.1:** 8 members, <$100k SC outstanding, <$25k annual fiat reserve, zero fiat redemption to members, all members CORA Nation co-religionists with signed affirmations. At this scale you are squarely inside multiple safe harbors. Above this — start the v0.2 elevation work BEFORE you cross any threshold, not after.
