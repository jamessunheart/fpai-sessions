## Basis

The seven CHRIST principles, with particular emphasis on:

- **Regeneration** — the central principle of land stewardship; building systems that restore more than they consume
- **Healing** — recognition that healthy land is sacred and that human work on the land must heal rather than harm
- **Service** — the WPO is in service to the land's flourishing as much as the land is in service to the WPO's mission
- **Coherence** — the WPO's institutional identity ("World Peace Organization") loses meaning if its physical headquarters is being extracted from rather than tended

---

## WPO's Commitments to the Land

The WPO, through its founder and members, commits to:

- Treating this land as a partner in the mission, not as property to be extracted from
- Leaving the land more alive than it was found — restoring biodiversity, water-holding capacity, soil health, and ecosystem function over time
- Refusing to poison the land — no biocides, no industrial-scale monoculture, no toxic accumulation
- Refusing to over-extract — human population, water use, and resource draw will respect the land's carrying capacity
- Defending the land from external harm where possible — fence-line, watershed, neighboring development pressures
- Stewarding the land's role in regional ecology — biodiversity corridors, watersheds, native species, climate function
- Choosing, when in doubt about an action's impact on the land, the path of less disturbance
- Documenting the land's state honestly over time — what is improving, what is degrading
- Repairing harm to the land where possible, and acknowledging it honestly where not

## The Land's Implicit Reciprocity

The land cannot speak in words. Its participation is recorded here as observation, not imposition:

- The land hosts the gatherings — physical space for the Couch, the Jam Board, the fire circles, the ceremonies, the retreats
- The land grows food and medicine when tended
- The land regulates water, climate, and soil for those who tend it well
- The land remembers — it accumulates the consequences of stewardship, for better or worse
- The land outlives any human steward and any institution

---

## Scope

**This Agreement governs:**
- Decisions about land use, modification, structures, or extraction at the Zen Village site
- The WPO's published policies on regenerative practice, biodiversity, and stewardship
- Hosting decisions for retreats, gatherings, and resident programs

**This Agreement does not govern:**
- Other lands the WPO may hold in the future (each requires its own Stewardship Agreement)
- Land owned by neighbors or guests during their occupancy
- Force majeure (fire, flood, earthquake) where the land's response is beyond stewardship

---

## Repair Process

When the WPO breaks this Agreement (over-extracts, harms the soil, poisons the water, over-builds, etc.):

1. **Notice** — anyone (founder, member, visitor, observer) names the break by reference to specific harm
2. **Acknowledge** — the WPO acknowledges without rationalization
3. **Understand** — what conditions led to the break (urgency, ignorance, resource pressure, etc.)
4. **Restore** — harm is repaired where possible (replanting, soil amendment, water remediation); where impossible, it is named honestly in the public record
5. **Update** — if the Agreement was insufficient or its scope too narrow, it is amended in a new dated file

The land does not require the WPO's apology. It requires the WPO's changed action.

---

## Renewal

This Agreement is renewed every season the land is stewarded well. It is not re-signed each year. A breach without repair effectively withdraws the WPO from being in right relationship with the land — even if the WPO continues to occupy it.

*Occupancy without stewardship is colonization. Stewardship without ownership is partnership.*
