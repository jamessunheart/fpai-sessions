## Section 5 — Pre-Engagement Checklist for James

Before sending the email above, James must verify the following. None of these is hard — each is a 5-minute check — but skipping them risks paying for an engagement that turns out to be a poor fit.

### A — Firm conflicts and current capacity (~10 min per firm)

- [ ] **Call or email the firm's intake line.** Confirm the firm currently accepts new client engagements; confirm conflicts-check turnaround time (boutiques typically 24-48 hrs; BigLaw 1-2 weeks).
- [ ] **Ask if a 1-2 hour scope engagement at $300-500/hr is workable.** Some firms have minimum-engagement floors ($2,500 retainer is common). If the firm has a floor higher than $1,000, this engagement doesn't fit and Option A or fallback should be reconsidered.
- [ ] **Ask about fixed-fee option.** A defined 1-2 hour scope is the kind of work that some boutiques will quote a fixed fee for; this caps cost risk.

### B — Verify firm depth on the specific deliverables (~15 min total)

- [ ] **Has the firm handled 508(c)(1)(A) structures specifically (not just generic 501(c)(3))?** Ask the intake to confirm by example or attorney bio.
- [ ] **Has the firm handled §4958 rebuttable-presumption documentation work?** Ask the intake to confirm; ask for an example without client-identifying details (e.g., "we've done this for a small religious community in California" suffices).
- [ ] **Has the firm worked with any community-currency, internal scrip, or church-scrip-like arrangements?** This is the hardest part of the engagement; if the answer is "no, but we'd analyze it from first principles," that's acceptable but should be flagged in the engagement letter so estimated hours can be revised if novel issues emerge.
- [ ] **Does the firm have any published articles or speaking engagements on relevant topics?** Pull 1-2 to confirm doctrinal alignment with the engagement scope. (Church Law Center's "501(c)(3) vs 508(c)(1)(A)" article is the right reference for Option A; Adler & Colvin has multiple published §4958 pieces; Caplin & Drysdale has decades of *Tax Notes* religious-org coverage.)

### C — References (~10 min)

- [ ] **Ask the firm for one or two religious-community client references** willing to confirm engagement quality. Boutiques generally have a few comfortable referring; BigLaw may decline citing confidentiality.
- [ ] **Where possible, check legal-directory ratings.** Martindale-Hubbell, Chambers, and Best Lawyers all list religious-org and exempt-org practitioners with peer reviews.

### D — Engagement letter terms (~10 min)

- [ ] **Confirm written engagement letter expected before any work begins.** No exceptions. Standard attorney-client privilege applies; scope-of-work is explicit; cap on hours is explicit; deliverables are explicit.
- [ ] **Confirm written memo deliverable** at engagement close (not just oral guidance). Even a 1-2 page memo is enough; oral-only engagements are recoverable but harder to use as the basis for future AI-council review or future counsel engagements.
- [ ] **Confirm conflict between this engagement and any future broader engagement is not foreclosed.** Some firms will not take a small scope if they think it's a tee-up for declining the bigger work later. Explicit framing: "If this engagement goes well, I plan to engage your firm or a peer firm for a broader 7-deliverable engagement at a higher budget; this is a sanity-pass first."
