## TL;DR

This pack is a serious, well-structured response to the prior CRITICAL gap — the PMA wall is now asserted *and* documented. The bifurcated authority architecture, §4958 rebuttable-presumption procedure, and hard-rule non-amendment locks are all present and coherent. The highest-risk remaining issue is the **independent-body gap**: the §4958 procedure is architecturally correct but operationally inert until a real, majority-independent Stewardship Council exists with named members who are verifiably independent of the Founding Steward — and that body does not exist yet. The biggest unaddressed structural gap is the **Founding Steward removal threshold** (9/10 of all Members), which could draw IRS scrutiny as effective irremovability; it needs counsel scrutiny against inurement doctrine. Do not open PMA Membership Agreements to the First Cohort until (a) counsel signs v1.0 and (b) at least three independent Stewardship Council members are seated and documented.

### 🔴 CRITICAL — Independent Stewardship Council does not yet exist: §4958 procedure is a paper tiger until it does

**Risk:**
The entire §4958 rebuttable-presumption defense collapses if the "independent Stewardship Council members" who approve Founding Steward compensation are not real, named, seated, and documentably independent. Treasury v0.10 §Governance Firewall states: *"Private inurement is the number one cause of intentional religious community legal destruction."* COI Policy §6.2 correctly requires that approving members be free of Related-Party ties to the Founding Steward. But as of v0.1, the Stewardship Council has not been constituted — the IRS Examination Readiness Index §2.9 shows all §4958 records as ❌ Open. If the Founding Steward begins receiving community-provision compensation (or if any value flows to the Sunheart Private Trust) before the independent Council is seated and before the §4958 procedure is completed, the rebuttable presumption is unavailable and any excess benefit is an unsecured §4958 first-tier (25%) or second-tier (200%) excise tax exposure.

**Corpus citation:**
remarkably-coherent-treasury-v0.10.md §Governance Firewall: *"Bifurcated Authority... The architecture is now operationally specified across ownership, control, settlement, measurement, governance, compensation, and compliance dimensions."* The architecture requires the Council to exist; the pack correctly identifies this as a precondition but does not establish a binding operational gate preventing compensation flows until the precondition is met.

**Suggested fix:**
Add a single hard-rule to Bylaws §12.02: *"No community-provision compensation to the Founding Steward or any Steward, and no allocation of Community assets above the §5.04 threshold, shall be authorized until at least three (3) independent Stewardship Council members have been seated, have completed initial COI disclosures per COI Policy §4.1, and have completed the §4958 procedure per COI Policy §6 for the relevant compensation determination."* This gate should also appear as a ❌ blocking precondition in the IRS Examination Readiness Index §3.2, not merely as a soft "owner: James" deliverable. COI Policy §6.5 already says this is a "hard activation precondition" — but it needs to be a Bylaws-level hard-rule with teeth.

### 🔴 CRITICAL — Founding Steward removal threshold (9/10 of all Members) likely makes removal practically impossible and may draw inurement attack

**Risk:**
Bylaws §14.01 (interim adoption by Founding Steward alone) + Pastoral Leadership Structure §2.7 (removal requires 9/10 of *all* Members, not merely those voting, plus unanimous Pastoral Council minus Founding Steward plus supermajority Stewardship Council) creates an effective irremovability during the early Community period when Members are few and the Founding Steward appoints the Pastoral Council. The IRS, in private-inurement examinations, looks at whether the founder controls the organization in fact regardless of formal governance rules. A removal threshold that requires — for example — 9 out of 10 Members when there are only 10-15 Members means a single loyalist blocks removal. This is the functional equivalent of founder veto over removal. An examiner will note this.

**Corpus citation:**
remarkably-coherent-treasury-v0.10.md §Governance Firewall: *"Private inurement is the number one cause of intentional religious community legal destruction. The IRS wins private inurement cases by showing that founders' personal interests were served through the religious entity."* The doc elsewhere acknowledges the risk; the removal threshold as drafted partially undermines the bifurcated-authority defense it is meant to support. Nothing in the corpus directly specifies a removal threshold, so this is a structural logic critique beyond the corpus.

**Suggested fix:**
Two options for counsel to choose between: (A) Lower the removal threshold to 2/3 of all Members (still high, still protective of the founding pastoral vision, but not practically irremovable) with the joint Pastoral + Stewardship Council gate preserved as a procedural check; or (B) Keep 9/10 but add a sunset: *"Until the Community reaches fifty (50) or more Members, the removal threshold is 2/3 of all Members; above fifty Members, the threshold rises to 3/4."* Option B respects the founding-stage reality while preventing a single-digit minority from making removal structurally impossible. Counsel should weigh in; flag specifically.

### 🔴 CRITICAL — Governing law clause in PMA Agreement is dangerously ambiguous: "State in which CORA Nation principally operates (or...Costa Rica)"

**Risk:**
PMA Membership Agreement §6.4 states the Agreement is governed by *"the laws of the State in which CORA Nation principally operates (or, if CORA Nation is held to be principally domiciled in Costa Rica, by Costa Rican law)."* This is unenforceable as drafted because: (i) there is no identified U.S. state — courts confronted with an ambiguous choice-of-law clause will apply the law of the forum, which may be the Member's home state, potentially a hostile securities or consumer-protection jurisdiction; (ii) the parenthetical Costa Rica option is effectively a court determination, not a party election — you cannot elect your governing law by reference to what a court might later "hold"; (iii) Members in multiple U.S. states and internationally will sign this Agreement, and each Member's state court may interpret the clause differently. A single-state choice (e.g., Wyoming or Texas, both historically favorable to religious associations and PMAs) is both legally cleaner and more protective.

**Corpus citation:**
The corpus does not directly address choice-of-law for membership agreements. This is general contract law, flagged here because it creates a gap in the PMA wall's enforceability. IRS Examination Readiness Index §3.3 notes that state registration survey and filings are open deliverables — the governing law determination feeds directly into that analysis.

**Suggested fix:**
Replace §6.4 with a single identified state: *"This Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of [Wyoming / Texas / as determined by counsel], without regard to conflicts-of-law principles, subject to the federal First Amendment and IRC protections that govern religious-community associations. To the extent CORA Nation maintains operations in Costa Rica, Costa Rica law governs solely the employment and residency matters arising from those operations."* Counsel determines the state; AI cannot. Flag for counsel engagement.

### 🟠 HIGH — Bylaws §14.01 interim adoption by Founding Steward alone creates a bootstrapping problem for religious-nonprofit legitimacy

**Risk:**
Bylaws §14.01 authorizes the Founding Steward alone to adopt the Bylaws as "interim charter" before Member ratification. This is standard for a new organization — but the doc is silent on two timing risks: (i) *how long* the interim period may last before Member ratification is required; and (ii) what happens if the Founding Steward begins exercising fiduciary authority under the interim Bylaws before independent Stewardship Council members are seated. During the interim period, the Founding Steward effectively holds *both* spiritual authority and all fiduciary authority (since the Stewardship Council doesn't exist yet), which is precisely the bifurcation failure the entire architecture is designed to prevent. An IRS examiner examining the period between Founding Steward signing and first Annual Meeting will see a period of undivided founder control — exactly the fact pattern that triggers private-inurement scrutiny.

**Corpus citation:**
remarkably-coherent-treasury-v0.10.md §Governance Firewall: *"Architectural separation between spiritual authority and secular fiduciary control is the structural defense."* The interim period creates a documented gap in that separation.

**Suggested fix:**
Add §14.01(b): *"The interim period shall not exceed one hundred eighty (180) days from the date of signing. During the interim period, no community-provision compensation, no allocation above the §5.04 threshold, no arm's-length transaction with a Related Party, and no disposition of Community assets shall be authorized until at least three (3) independent Stewardship Council members have been seated per §12.02 CRITICAL fix above. The Founding Steward shall use best efforts to seat the Stewardship Council within sixty (60) days of signing."* This creates an on-the-record commitment that limits the bifurcation gap.

### 🟠 HIGH — §4958 procedure requires "at least three sources" of comp data but no minimum quality standard is specified

**Risk:**
COI Policy §6.1 requires comparable-compensation data from "at least three (3) of the following sources" (BLS, peer-org surveys, Form 990 data, published compensation studies, counsel-provided benchmarks). This is formally correct as a checklist — but §4958 rebuttable presumption under Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6(c)(2) requires the data to be "appropriate data as to comparability." Courts and the IRS have rejected rebuttable-presumption claims where the comparables were cherry-picked, insufficiently similar in scope/role/size, or where the council relied on summary characterizations rather than actual data. The Policy as drafted permits the Comparable-Compensation Memorandum to be auto-generated from a "registry" (§6.4) without specifying what the registry must contain or how the comparables must be validated.

**Corpus citation:**
remarkably-coherent-treasury-v0.10.md §Governance Firewall and Brand Tok v0.4 Addendum B.5 (§4958 Rebuttable-Presumption Documentation Template) both require this procedure. The corpus does not specify data quality standards — this is a gap in both the corpus and the pack.

**Suggested fix:**
Add to COI Policy §6.1: *"The Comparable-Compensation Memorandum shall: (i) identify at least three (3) organizations that are comparable in religious-community type, size (by Members and revenue), and geographic region; (ii) describe the scope of role and time commitment for each comparable position; (iii) document the source of each data point with enough specificity that an independent reviewer could verify it; and (iv) be reviewed by external counsel before first use and annually thereafter. The Allocation Platform registry shall capture all fields required by this standard."* Also: expressly note that auto-generation supplements but does not replace independent-Council review of actual data.

### 🟠 HIGH — Bylaws §5.04 allocation threshold is not inflation-indexed and the USD $25,000 cap may be too low relative to Costa Rica operating reality

**Risk:**
Bylaws §5.04 requires Member approval for any single allocation exceeding *"the lesser of (i) 10% of unrestricted Community assets at the start of the fiscal year or (ii) USD $25,000."* The USD $25,000 floor creates two problems: (a) for an early-stage Community with $100,000 in unrestricted assets, the 10% trigger fires at $10,000 — routine operational expenses (a retreat, a ceremony, a month's land maintenance) will repeatedly require Member votes, creating governance friction that may impair operational continuity; and (b) the fixed dollar amount will erode with inflation, gradually tightening the governance trigger without any Member decision to do so. Neither problem is insurmountable, but both should be addressed.

**Corpus citation:**
Treasury v0.10 §What Needs to Be Formalized references the need for "Published community governance documentation" and "Auditable ceremony schedules, teaching records, community decisions, pastoral care" — governance friction that prevents documenting routine decisions undermines the examination-readiness record.

**Suggested fix:**
Consider adding: (i) a separate "routine operational" carve-out for categories of expenditure that are pre-approved in the annual budget (e.g., ceremony costs, land maintenance, pastoral stipends within §4958-approved ranges), with a cap per category; (ii) inflation indexing: *"The USD $25,000 figure shall be adjusted annually by the CPI-U, rounded to the nearest $1,000, effective each fiscal year start."* The goal is to keep Member votes on *material* allocations, not routine ones.

### 🟠 HIGH — Bylaws §5.05 makes the Allocation Platform spec a Bylaw-incorporated document before the spec exists — creates a forward-reference governance loop

**Risk:**
Bylaws §5.05 states that Allocation Platform "protocol-layer constants" are "NOT bylaw-amendable per §12.02." But the Allocation Platform spec does not yet exist — it is paused pending this Constitutional Pack's completion. This creates a forward-reference problem: the Bylaws lock in rules from a document that hasn't been finalized, and when that document is eventually ratified, it will determine what the hard-rules are. If the Allocation Platform spec v1.0 contains rules inconsistent with Bylaws §12.02 as drafted, there's a circular dependency. More concerning: Members ratifying the Bylaws at the first Annual Meeting will be voting to lock in rules from a document they haven't yet seen.

**Corpus citation:**
This is a structural drafting issue beyond the corpus. The corpus (IRS Examination Readiness Index §3.5) acknowledges the Allocation Platform is downstream and paused — the governance loop is a natural consequence of the sequencing.

**Suggested fix:**
Add a qualifier to Bylaws §5.05: *"The Allocation Platform's protocol-layer constants are incorporated as hard-rules under §12.02 upon and from the date of Member ratification of the Allocation Platform spec per a future Member supermajority vote. Until such ratification, §5.05 establishes the structural intent but does not yet incorporate specific constants."* This prevents the Bylaws from locking in rules Members haven't reviewed while preserving the architectural commitment.

### 🟡 MEDIUM — Bylaws §5.07 Founding Steward doctrinal veto overridable by 3/4 supermajority — but what qualifies as "doctrinal"?

**Risk:**
Bylaws §5.07 gives the Founding Steward a veto over "Canon amendments or doctrinal matters" but does not define where the doctrinal/fiduciary boundary lies. Bylaws §14.03 acknowledges this boundary and says the two Councils resolve it jointly — but the veto can be exercised before the joint-resolution process runs. A Founding Steward facing a supermajority vote on a fiduciary matter (e.g., reducing the Founding Steward's compensation) could claim the matter implicates doctrinal authority (e.g., "the community-provision model is a doctrinal commitment") and exercise the veto to delay or block. This is not a bad-faith hypothetical — it is a natural institutional dynamic. The absence of a definition creates a gap that could be exploited and that an IRS examiner would flag as governance-structure evidence of founder control over fiduciary matters.

**Corpus citation:**
remarkably-coherent-treasury-v0.10.md §Governance Firewall distinguishes between spiritual/doctrinal/pastoral authority and secular fiduciary authority — the corpus draws the distinction but does not define where it lies in specific cases.

**Suggested fix:**
Add a definitional provision to §5.07 or Article I: *"For purposes of this veto, 'doctrinal matters' means matters directly concerning: (i) the interpretation of Canon; (ii) the conduct of ceremony and sacramental practice; (iii) the recognition or removal of persons from pastoral roles. 'Doctrinal matters' do NOT include: (i) Steward compensation; (ii) allocation or disposition of Community assets; (iii) arm's-length transactions with Brand LLCs; (iv) adoption or amendment of the Conflict of Interest Policy; or (v) any matter within the exclusive authority of the Stewardship Council under §3.02. In the event of dispute, the Stewardship Council's determination of the fiduciary/doctrinal character of a matter is final."*

### 🟡 MEDIUM — PMA Membership Agreement §6.3 "limited external resort" clause may not be enforceable in all states, especially for non-doctrinal claims

**Risk:**
PMA Membership Agreement §6.3 requires exhaustion of internal religious-community dispute resolution before resort to civil courts except for limited categories (criminal conduct, civil rights, matters not subject to religious-community resolution, third-party disputes). This is a standard internal-dispute-resolution clause and is appropriate. However: (i) in several states (notably California), pre-dispute agreements to exhaust internal religious processes before filing civil claims have been challenged as unconscionable where the power imbalance between the institution and the individual is significant; (ii) the carve-out for "matters that are by law not subject to religious-community resolution" (§6.3(iii)) is broad enough to largely swallow the rule; (iii) the clause does not specify a maximum timeframe for internal resolution before civil resort is permitted, which could be used to delay a Member's access to courts indefinitely.

**Corpus citation:**
The corpus does not directly address enforceability of internal-dispute clauses. This is general contract / first amendment law.

**Suggested fix:**
Add: (i) a maximum internal-resolution timeline (e.g., *"The internal resolution process shall be completed within ninety (90) days of initiation; if not resolved within that period, the Member may seek external resolution without further obligation to exhaust internal process"*); (ii) an explicit carve-out for EEOC and state civil rights filings that cannot be pre-dispute waived; (iii) note in §6.4 that the governing-law clause (once fixed per CRITICAL issue above) determines which state's unconscionability doctrine applies.

### 🟡 MEDIUM — Pastoral Leadership Structure §2.7 removal provision for Founding Steward requires "entire Pastoral Council excluding the Founding Steward" — but the Founding Steward appoints the initial Pastoral Council

**Risk:**
The Founding Steward appoints the initial Pastoral Council per §3.1 (subject to Member ratification at first Annual Meeting). Until that ratification, the Pastoral Council consists of persons the Founding Steward chose. The §2.7 removal process requires the "entire Pastoral Council excluding the Founding Steward" — meaning the very persons the Founding Steward appointed must unanimously agree to remove the Founding Steward. This is the same bootstrapping problem as the interim-adoption issue, applied to removal rather than governance. An IRS examiner will note the circularity.

**Corpus citation:**
remarkably-coherent-treasury-v0.10.md §Governance Firewall: *"The IRS wins private inurement cases by showing that founders' personal interests were served through the religious entity."* Founder-appointed Council required to unanimously initiate founder removal is a fact pattern that will draw scrutiny.

**Suggested fix:**
Add: *"For purposes of §2.7, Pastoral Members who were initially appointed by the Founding Steward and have not yet been independently ratified by Member supermajority are not eligible to vote on the removal of the Founding Steward. Removal may only be initiated after Member ratification of the Pastoral Council per Bylaws §14.02."* This creates a bootstrapping gate that is honest about the founding-stage limitation.

### 🟡 MEDIUM — Admission & Exit Procedures §11.3 "material foreign-securities... exposure" referral to Counsel Liaison assumes the Counsel Liaison has the expertise to flag it — but that role is internal

**Risk:**
§11.3 states the Community shall not admit a Member whose participation would create "a material foreign-securities, foreign-tax, or foreign-money-transmission exposure" and that "the Counsel Liaison shall flag any such case." The Counsel Liaison is an internal Officer (Bylaws Article IX) — a Steward, not an attorney. This provision effectively relies on an untrained internal officer to identify securities law exposure, which is precisely the kind of judgment that requires licensed counsel. Getting this determination wrong is a failure mode: a Member admitted without the correct analysis who later claims securities violations could point to this provision as evidence the Community recognized the risk but used an unqualified reviewer.

**Corpus citation:**
The corpus does not directly address the Counsel Liaison role's qualifications. This is a structural design observation.

**Suggested fix:**
Either: (i) add *"The Counsel Liaison shall refer any such case to external counsel before the Membership determination is made"*; or (ii) create a categorical rule rather than a case-by-case analysis: *"Members who are residents of [list of high-risk jurisdictions with active crypto-securities enforcement, e.g., EU member states subject to MiCA, SEC-active US states] shall not receive Allocation Weight activation until the Allocation Platform spec has been reviewed by counsel for that jurisdiction."* A bright-line rule is more defensible than discretionary Counsel Liaison review.

### 🟡 MEDIUM — Records Protocol §5.3 permits sharing aggregated statistics at the Annual Meeting including "total minted, decayed, transferred (by transaction type)" — aggregated flows may still support secondary-market inference

**Risk:**
The Records Protocol's leaderboard suppression of absolute individual balances (§5.1-5.2) is correctly designed. But §5.3 permits aggregate totals at the Annual Meeting including "total minted, decayed, transferred (by transaction type)." If the Community is small (e.g., 10-15 Members at the first Annual Meeting), aggregate statistics may be de-anonymizable — a Member who knows their own balance and the aggregate can infer others' balances. This is a weaker secondary-market signal than individual leaderboard data, but it is still a signal, and the prior Counsel critique flagged leaderboard data specifically because of secondary-market characterization risk.

**Corpus citation:**
Prior Counsel critique of Allocation Platform v0.1 (2026-05-12) HIGH #4 flagged the price-signal risk of public absolute balances. The Records Protocol correctly responds to that critique for individual data; the aggregation risk is a second-order version of the same issue.

**Suggested fix:**
Add to §5.3: *"Aggregate statistics shall not be presented to the extent they would permit a reasonable observer, knowing their own balance, to infer the balance of any other individual Member, or to establish a market-observable price for Allocation Weight. Where the Community has fewer than [50] Members, aggregate statistics shall be presented only in ranges (e.g., 'between 1,000 and 5,000 units minted this quarter') rather than precise totals, until the Community reaches a size where de-anonymization risk is negligible."*

## Missing / Unaddressed

1. **State charitable solicitation registration.** The pack does not address whether CORA Nation is required to register as a charitable organization with state attorneys general before soliciting donations. Brand Tok v0.4 Addendum C identified a state charitable solicitation registration survey as a Counsel deliverable, but the Constitutional Pack does not flag this as a precondition on CORA Nation's religious-community operations (not just Brand LLC operations). Many states (notably California, New York, Pennsylvania, Illinois, Florida, Virginia, Washington) require registration before charitable solicitation regardless of federal §508(c)(1)(A) status. The IRS Examination Readiness Index §3.3 lists this as a gap; it should be elevated to a gate, not a soft deliverable.

2. **Anti-money-laundering / Know-Your-Member procedures.** The pack has no AML/KYC reference. Coherent Credit minting and the eventual Allocation Platform, combined with international Members (§11.3), create at minimum a FinCEN analysis obligation. If any Member is in a FATF high-risk jurisdiction, the Community's acceptance of funds from that Member (even as membership contributions) needs AML analysis. This is currently entirely unaddressed.

3. **UBIT analysis for any "religious-community economic activity."** Treasury v0.10 §What Is Not describes the CORA economy as a religious practice. But UBIT (Unrelated Business Income Tax) applies even to §508(c)(1)(A) churches for trade-or-business income that
