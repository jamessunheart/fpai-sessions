## §1 — Purpose

IRC §4958 imposes excise taxes on "excess benefit transactions" between a §501(c)(3) (or §508(c)(1)(A) church) organization and a "disqualified person." The statute provides a **rebuttable presumption of reasonableness** if the organization follows the three-element procedure of Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6.

This document codifies that procedure for any issuance of Soultime Credits (SC) to a disqualified person in amounts exceeding the standard ministerial recognition schedule. Following the procedure faithfully shifts the burden of proof on any §4958 challenge from the organization to the IRS.

## §2 — Who is a "disqualified person" for CORA Nation v0.1

Per IRC §4958(f)(1) and Treas. Reg. §53.4958-3, a disqualified person is anyone who, at any time during the 5 years before the transaction, was in a position to exercise substantial influence over the organization. For CORA Nation v0.1, this includes:

| Person / role | Why disqualified |
|---|---|
| **James Stinson** (Founding Steward) | Founder · convening authority · charism-holder |
| **CORA Nation Board members** | Governance authority |
| **Pastoral Council elders** | Doctrinal-spiritual authority |
| **Family members or business partners of any of the above** | §4958(f)(1)(B)–(D) relatives + 35%-controlled entities |
| **Substantial contributors** (any single contributor of >2% of total contributions in the prior 5 years, where total is >$5k) | §4958(f)(1)(C) substantial-contributor test |

For the v0.1 founding cohort (8 members), the disqualified-person set is presumed to include: James, any Board member or Pastoral Council elder upon their appointment, and any family/spouse of the same. Standard ministerial recognition issuance to non-disqualified-person co-religionists (the other founding-cohort members) does **not** trigger this procedure.

## §3 — When the procedure is triggered

**The procedure is required whenever:**

- An SC issuance to a disqualified person exceeds the **standard ministerial recognition schedule** (defined below in §4); OR
- A pattern of multiple smaller issuances to the same disqualified person within a 12-month period cumulatively exceeds the standard schedule; OR
- Any non-standard transaction with a disqualified person involving SC, fiat, property, or other consideration (e.g., asset transfer, compensation arrangement, lending against future SC) is contemplated.

**The procedure is NOT required for:**

- Standard ministerial recognition within the schedule (e.g., 10 SC seed grant on Membership Covenant signature applied uniformly across the cohort);
- SC issuance to a member who is not a disqualified person;
- Routine refund of out-of-pocket expenses substantiated by receipts, at-or-below FMV.

## §5 — The three-element procedure (Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6)

For any triggering transaction, the Board shall complete **all three** elements before authorizing the issuance:

### Element 1 — Comparable data

Obtain and consider information sufficient to determine whether the proposed transaction's benefit is at or below what would be paid for comparable ministerial recognition in comparable religious-community contexts.

**Required:** comparable-data documentation from **at least three** analogous religious-community recognition contexts. Examples of acceptable comparables:

- Hutterite colony stipend / labor-credit schedules (publicly available in academic literature)
- Bruderhof common-purse allocation documentation
- Catholic religious-order living-stipend schedules
- Other §501(c)(3) or §508(c)(1)(A) church ministerial-honorarium ranges (e.g., from ECFA-published surveys)
- Internal CORA Nation comparable transactions with non-disqualified-person members for substantively similar service

**Special note for §4958(c)(6) safe harbor:** Treas. Reg. §53.4958-6(c)(2)(ii) provides a small-organization safe harbor when organization gross receipts are < $1M and at least three comparable contexts are documented. CORA Nation v0.1 falls under this safe-harbor scale.

### Element 2 — Independent approval

Approval by **majority vote of CORA Nation Board members who are themselves NOT disqualified persons** with respect to the proposed transaction.

**Mechanics:**
- The disqualified person who is the subject of the transaction recuses from the deliberation and the vote;
- Any other Board member who is a related party (per §4958(f)(1)(B)–(D)) also recuses;
- The remaining "independent" Board members deliberate, document their consideration of the comparable data, and vote;
- Majority of the independent Board members must approve;
- If there are not enough independent Board members to constitute a majority of the seated Board, the procedure cannot be completed and the transaction shall not proceed under §4958-presumption safe harbor (a separate Phase-2 attorney engagement is required before any such transaction).

For v0.1 (founding cohort) the Board shall include **at least two non-disqualified-person seats** before any disqualified-person SC issuance procedure is exercised. If two are not seated, the Board shall fill them as a precondition.

### Element 3 — Contemporaneous documentation

The Board shall document the deliberation and decision in **contemporaneous written records**, signed and dated within **60 days** of the approval (or by the date of the next regularly scheduled Board meeting, whichever is earlier).

The contemporaneous record must include:

- Date and time of deliberation;
- Names of Board members present, names of recused parties, names of voters;
- Terms of the transaction (recipient, SC amount, service recognized, any other consideration);
- A clear statement of the comparable data considered (citations / sources);
- The Board's basis for finding the transaction reasonable (i.e., why the proposed benefit is at or below FMV in comparable religious-community contexts);
- The vote tally;
- Signatures of approving Board members and the recording secretary.

Records retained for **not less than seven years** per Canon §4.6 and the Records & Privacy Protocol.

### Template C — Annual independent review (per `BANK_V01_LEDGER_CHARTER.md` §6 procedural integrity)

Once per calendar year, a Board member who is **not a disqualified person** with respect to **any** of the transactions under review shall:

1. Pull all Ledger entries with `disqualified_person: true` from the prior year;
2. Verify each entry has a `4958_procedure: completed` flag;
3. Verify each entry has a corresponding `board_minute_ref` and signed contemporaneous record;
4. Verify the three comparable-data sources are retrievable from records;
5. Verify no pattern of cumulative issuance has crept above the standard schedule for any disqualified person without triggering the procedure;
6. File the annual review as a Board record, retained 7 years.

If any defect is identified, the review-Board-member reports to the full Board within 30 days for remediation.

## §8 — Failure modes and corrective action

| Failure mode | Detection | Corrective action |
|---|---|---|
| Issuance to disqualified person without triggering the procedure | Annual independent review (Template C step 5) or interim audit | Halt further such issuance · convene Board · either reverse the issuance or complete the procedure retroactively (with caveat — retroactive completion does not earn safe-harbor presumption; consider Phase-2 attorney) |
| Independent-Board-majority cannot be achieved | At Element 2 step | Do NOT proceed. Convene Board to fill non-disqualified-person seats. Defer transaction until majority is achievable, OR engage Phase-2 attorney. |
| Comparable data insufficient (< 3 contexts) | At Element 1 step | Defer transaction. Counsel substrate (AI) commissioned to surface additional comparables. If still insufficient, scale the transaction down to within the standard schedule, OR engage Phase-2 attorney for sui-generis valuation. |
| Documentation not signed within 60 days | At signing | Sign late and document the delay reason in the record. Safe-harbor presumption is at risk; consider Phase-2 attorney consultation if the transaction is material. |
| Cumulative issuance pattern not noticed | Annual review | Reset trigger threshold tracking; flag the recipient for elevated scrutiny going forward. |
