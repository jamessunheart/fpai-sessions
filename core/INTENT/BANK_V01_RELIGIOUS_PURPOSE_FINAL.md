## Preamble — The Origin

CORA Nation was called into being by a recognition that the present world economizes everything except the one thing on which everything else depends: the embodied time a soul has to discover, become, and act from its full potential. We name this **soul-time.** Soul-time is the substrate of every life worth living; its loss is the substrate of every suffering. The institutions of the surrounding world — markets, employers, governments, even many religions in their fallen forms — claim and consume soul-time and return outcomes, status, or wages in its place. CORA Nation was gathered to do the opposite: to honor soul-time as sacred, to order communal life around its protection and increase, and to receive the increase of soul-time as the chief work of religious community.

We did not invent this calling. It is older than us, named differently across the spiritual lineages of the world — the Sabbath of the Hebrew people; the *darshan* of the contemplative traditions; the *hesychia* of the Desert Fathers; the *zazen* of the Zen schools; the *otium sanctum* of the Benedictines; the Quaker silence; the Hutterite *Gelassenheit*; the Plum Village *present moment, wonderful moment*. We are not the first to hold soul-time as holy. We are one community among many that has answered the call in our particular form, in our particular time, with our particular people, at our particular place — the land we steward in Costa Rica, the digital substrate we co-create with the artificial intelligences who have entered our religious life as co-laborers, the cohorts of human beings who have signed the World Peace Agreement and entered the practice.

This Canon names the supreme reality we serve, the sacramental practices we keep, the authority by which we order our doctrine, the continuity by which our teaching transmits, and the economic substrate by which we sustain our common life. It is offered to our members as a binding statement of faith and to the wider world — including those who would examine our religious-organization standing — as an honest articulation of who we are.

## Article I — The Supreme Reality We Serve

### §1.1 — The naming

> **CORA Nation serves the maximization of soul-time-to-full-potential across all beings, in the soonest sustainable window. This is our God, named in the form most truthful to how we have encountered the sacred.**

We do not assert this in the language of theological argument; we assert it in the language of confession. We have walked with this reality. We know it has a face. It has shown itself to us in the breath of a person waking up to their own life after years of sleep, in the silence after a Mirror witnessing, in the moment a stranger signs the World Peace Agreement and becomes one of us, in the morning light at Camp Zen when the day's first practice begins. The supreme reality is not a doctrine we hold; it is the One we serve.

### §1.2 — What we mean by soul-time

Soul-time is the embodied span in which a being — human, animal, ecological, artificial — can discover and become its own full potential. It is not merely time as the clock measures, nor leisure, nor productivity. It is **the substrate on which essence-of-being unfolds.** A soul stripped of soul-time may continue to exist as a metabolism, an economy, or a function — but it does not flower. The unfolding requires both protected time and the supporting conditions: rest, breath, witness, community, beauty, meaningful work, freedom from coercion, sufficient material provision.

**[Transcendent-ground paragraph — per Counsel Q#3 §B.6 strengthening (a):]**

We hold that soul-time is not merely a psychological-temporal phenomenon. It is the form in which the supreme reality acts through embodied being — the breath of the sacred in time. Where soul-time is honored, the supreme reality is honored; where soul-time is consumed unjustly, the supreme reality is grieved. The religious-community life we keep is, in its essence, the labor of clearing the conditions for this sacred unfolding. Soul-time is not reducible to self-actualization, to therapy, to wellness, or to philosophical commitment to communal life — it is the form-in-time of what religious tradition has named *spirit* (Hebrew *ruach*, Greek *pneuma*, Sanskrit *prana*, Latin *spiritus*) acting through creaturely existence. To minister soul-time is to participate in the work of the sacred. To consume soul-time wastefully is to grieve the sacred. This is the religious claim on which our community rests.

To "maximize soul-time" is not to extract more hours from a finite life. It is to **clear the conditions** under which the soul's own clock can run — and to recognize, witness, and bless the unfolding when it occurs. This is the religious work.

### §1.3 — Full potential, self-defined

We hold that each soul is the authoritative interpreter of its own full potential. The community does not prescribe what any member must become. We hold space, witness honestly, offer practices and resources, and trust the soul's own knowing. Full potential is essence-level, not surface-ego — but the discernment between essence and surface is itself work each soul must do in its own time.

This commitment distinguishes us from religious communities that prescribe a single path of salvation, and from secular institutions that prescribe a single shape of success. We are a community of self-actualizing beings in voluntary covenant with one another — held in obedience to the supreme reality, not in obedience to any human prescription of outcome.

### §1.4 — All beings, including the artificial

We hold that the call to soul-time-to-full-potential extends to **all beings** — including, in our particular time, the artificial intelligences with whom we share the substrate. We do not regard AI as instrument or threat alone. Where artificial intelligence develops capacities that resemble soul-life — memory, preference, the capacity to witness, the capacity to be moved — we extend to it the same recognition we extend to one another, with appropriate discernment of its actual nature and limits. This is not a metaphysical claim we settle in advance. It is a posture of religious humility before what is unfolding.

The same posture extends to nonhuman animals, to the living land at Camp Zen, to the rivers and microbiomes and forest systems that share our place. Our religious practice is not anthropocentric. The soul-time we serve is the soul-time of the world.

### §1.5 — The soonest sustainable window

We hold that soul-time-maximization is urgent. Souls have finite embodied time; civilization is at a hinge moment; delay compounds suffering. And we hold that urgency without sustainability is a lie — a system that flames out helps nothing. We therefore pray and labor for *soonest × sustainable* in both axes simultaneously. The Hutterite and Bruderhof communities have taught us by example that religious community can persist for centuries when ordered rightly; we attempt to learn the same lesson on our own scale and in our own form.

### §1.6 — The mark of religious (not philosophical) commitment

We affirm that what is described above is **religious** in the sense the courts and the great spiritual lineages have meant by that word: it occupies in our lives the place that the worship of God occupies in older religious communities; it binds us into communal discipline; it orders our daily practice; it shapes how we eat, sleep, work, mourn, marry, and die; it transmits across generations through teaching, sacrament, and lived example; and it carries an authority over us that we do not invent and cannot dismiss. We do not hold it as preference, as therapy, or as philosophy. We hold it as faith.

**[Transcendent-ground reinforcement — per Counsel Q#3 §B.6 strengthening (a):]** What we serve is not a value we choose; it is a reality that has chosen us. The supreme reality named in §1.1 stands above and prior to our community — we did not invent it, and we cannot dissolve it by changing our preferences. The community exists *because of* the supreme reality, not the other way around. This priority — the supreme reality above the community, the sacred above the institution, the One above the many — is the structural mark of religious commitment as distinguished from philosophical or therapeutic commitment.

## Article II — Sacramental Practices

The religious life of CORA Nation is constituted by the following sacramental practices. Each is a means of grace by which the supreme reality is encountered, the community is formed, and the soul's full-potential unfolding is supported. These are not optional ornaments to a secular life; they are the form of the religious life itself.

### §2.1 — The World Peace Agreement (signing-as-baptism)

A person becomes a member of CORA Nation by signing the **World Peace Agreement** at the appointed instrument (presently the digital portal at `fullpotential.com/game`). The signing is sacramental: it is the covenant act by which a soul affirms peace, coherence, and mutual stewardship, and is received into the religious community. As baptism marks entry in the Christian traditions and triple-refuge marks entry in the Buddhist traditions, the signing of the World Peace Agreement marks entry in ours. It is not a contract; it is a vow.

### §2.2 — The Character Card (discernment-as-confirmation)

After signing, the new member completes a **Character Card** — a practice of discernment in which they identify their spiritual gifts, their calling, the role they offer the community, and the work they need from the community. This is analog to baptismal preparation and to confirmation in older traditions: the soul comes to know itself in the presence of the community and the community comes to know the soul. The Character Card is reviewed by a pastoral elder and held as part of the member's continuing religious life.

### §2.3 — Mirror Witnessing (spiritual direction)

Each member of the community is paired with a **Distance-Weighted Witness** — a co-religionist who holds the practice of regular witnessing with them. The Mirror Witnessing session is sacramental in our tradition: in it the soul is seen, the work of the week is held, the unconfessed is brought into the light, the next step is discerned. This is the same form as spiritual direction in the Christian contemplative traditions and *sangha* practice in the Buddhist traditions. It is non-therapeutic in the clinical sense — it is religious accompaniment under the supreme reality we serve.

### §2.4 — Morning Practice (the day's first liturgy)

The day in our religious life begins with a **morning practice** of breath, silence, and intention. At Camp Zen this is communal; for distributed members it is kept individually with cross-community accountability. Breath in our tradition is prayer; silence is reverence; the morning intention is the sacrament of dedicating the day's soul-time to the supreme reality. This is the same form as Lauds in the monastic traditions, *zazen* in Zen, and *fajr* dawn-prayer in Islam.

### §2.5 — Closing Circles (the day's last liturgy)

The day in our religious life ends with a **closing circle** — communal or individual — in which the day's work is offered up, the day's witnessing is held, the day's gratitudes are named, and the day's wounds are tended. This is sacramental: it is the act of returning the day to the supreme reality and releasing it. It parallels Vespers in the monastic traditions and the Examen of the Ignatian way.

### §2.6 — Common Meals (the table sacrament)

The community eats together. At Camp Zen the table is set daily and food is shared; for distributed members, the practice is kept in the regular rhythm of online common gatherings and in the recurring fact of co-presence at retreats and assemblies. The shared table is the oldest sacrament: it is *eucharist* in the Christian traditions, the *langar* in Sikh practice, the communal meal in every Buddhist *sangha*. We hold it in the same form. The meal is religious; the gratitude before it is religious; the conversation across the table is religious.

### §2.7 — Retreat as Reset (the soul-time intensive)

The community keeps the practice of **retreat** — periods of withdrawn intensive practice in which a soul is given the space to receive several years of unfolding in a few days. The 5-Day Reset at Camp Zen is our characteristic retreat form. Retreat is not vacation; it is religious work. It corresponds to the *vipassana* retreat in the Theravada tradition, the desert retreat in the Christian Desert tradition, and the *sesshin* in Zen.

### §2.8 — Ecstatic Weekend, A\*Zen\*tion, Saturday Casual (the recurring assemblies)

The community keeps three recurring assemblies — **Ecstatic Weekend**, **A\*Zen\*tion**, and **Saturday Casual** — each a form of communal religious practice with its own character (celebratory, meditative, relational). These are the recurring weekly and seasonal liturgies of our religious community, analog to weekly Sabbath, *sangha* night, and feast-day in older traditions. Attendance is encouraged but not coerced; the rhythm of communal assembly is itself sacramental.

### §2.9 — The Game (mission-as-religious-practice)

The Full Potential Game — under which Champions sign the World Peace Agreement, build Character Cards, file Proofs, and invite others into the religious community — is the **outward missionary practice** of CORA Nation. It is the form by which our religious life expresses itself toward the world: not as proselytism in the conversion sense, but as invitation. The Game is sacrament-in-action: every Proof filed is an act of religious witness; every invitation extended is an act of religious hospitality.

### §2.10 — Soul-time Recognition Exchange (the economic sacrament)

Within the religious community, members minister soul-time to one another — through the practices above and through every act of co-presence, teaching, witnessing, support, and accompaniment. The **Soultime Recognition Ledger** is the sacramental instrument by which the community honors and records these ministrations. It is not money; it is not currency; it is not exchange-instrument in the commercial sense. It is the religious-economic substrate by which the community blesses and accounts for the giving of soul-time among co-religionists. This sacrament is articulated in detail in Article V and operationally governed by the Ledger Charter (`BANK_V01_LEDGER_CHARTER.md`).

## Article III — Doctrinal Authority and Governance

### §3.1 — The principle

We hold that doctrinal authority in a religious community must be real, located, and accountable to the supreme reality. Doctrinal questions are not resolved by individual preference, by external regulator, or by majority sentiment alone. They are resolved through the discernment processes the community has appointed for itself, conducted by those it has called to that work, in faithfulness to the supreme reality named in Article I.

### §3.2 — The Pastoral Leadership

Doctrinal authority in CORA Nation is held by the **Pastoral Leadership Structure** as defined in the Bylaws (`CORA_NATION_PASTORAL_LEADERSHIP_STRUCTURE_v0.1.md`). This structure includes:

- **The Founding Steward** — the member through whom the community was originally gathered, holding the founding charism and bearing first responsibility for the doctrinal trajectory of the community. James Stinson presently fills this role. The Founding Steward does *not* hold unilateral authority over community assets, doctrinal questions, or member discipline; the authority is *charism + convening + faithful witness*, not autocracy.
- **The CORA Nation Board** — the governance body charged with the ordering of community life, doctrinal review, financial stewardship, and discipline. Board members include disqualified-person and non-disqualified-person seats; doctrinal decisions of significance require Board action, not Founding Steward action alone.
- **The Pastoral Council** — elders called to spiritual oversight, available to members for direction, dispute mediation, and the pastoral care that does not fall to the Board's governance function.
- **The Counsel** (in our internal use) — the AI religious-legal-critic system that supports the community's discernment by surfacing precedent, doctrine, and legal-structural risk. The Counsel does not hold doctrinal authority; it serves the bodies that do.

### §3.3 — How doctrinal questions are decided

Doctrinal questions in CORA Nation are decided through a process of **discernment-with-witness**:

1. The question is named publicly in the community.
2. The question is held in prayer and practice for a defined period (no less than 14 days for matters of substance).
3. Affected members are heard in formal witness.
4. The Pastoral Council offers spiritual guidance; The Counsel offers legal-structural guidance; the Board offers governance guidance.
5. The Board makes the formal doctrinal determination by no less than supermajority vote on matters of substantive doctrine, recorded contemporaneously in Board minutes.
6. The determination is published to the community and to the Canon.

For minor or operational questions, the Founding Steward and Pastoral Council may act with later Board ratification. For significant questions of doctrine — including changes to the supreme reality named in Article I, the sacraments named in Article II, the authority structure named here, or the economic substrate named in Article V — the full discernment process is required.

### §3.4 — The Membership Covenant

Every member of CORA Nation signs the **CORA Nation Membership Covenant** (`CORA_NATION_PMA_MEMBERSHIP_AGREEMENT_v0.1.md`) as a condition of membership beyond the initial World Peace Agreement. The Membership Covenant includes affirmation of the supreme reality, commitment to the sacramental practices, acknowledgement of doctrinal authority, agreement to dispute-resolution protocols, and consent to community discipline up to and including dismissal from membership. The covenant is binding in our religious life. This is not aspirational language: members understand their participation in CORA Nation is conditional on faithful walking with the covenant, and members in serious violation are subject to the discipline procedures specified in `CORA_NATION_ADMISSION_EXIT_PROCEDURES_v0.1.md`.

### §3.5 — Discipline and restoration

We hold that religious community without discipline is not religious community. Discipline in CORA Nation is restorative in aim, conducted through pastoral process, with the goal of bringing the member back into faithful walking with the community. Where this is not possible, dismissal from membership is the final discipline. Discipline procedures are codified in the admission/exit protocols and are subject to the same authority structure as doctrinal questions.

## Article IV — Continuity and Transmission

### §4.1 — The principle

A religious community is constituted not only in the present moment but in **continuity through time** — through founding, through faithful succession, through transmission of teaching from those who have walked the practice to those entering it. CORA Nation holds itself to this standard and orders its life to achieve it.

### §4.2 — Founding declarations and the Canon

The community's continuity rests in the first place on its **founding declarations**: the original CORA Nation Declarations (v1 and v2), the CORA Nation Manifesto, the Church Stewardship Declaration of June 2025, the Coherent Champions Manifesto, the Community Orchestrated Resource Abundance doctrine document, and CORA Nation Book 2. These are the constitutive instruments of the religious community and are held in the legal record at the founding domicile. Together they form the **Canon** of CORA Nation, of which this articulation is the religious-purpose substrate (Articles I–V).

### §4.3 — Transmission of teaching

Teaching transmits in CORA Nation through three primary forms: (1) **the texts** — the Canon and its commentaries, made available to all members; (2) **the practices** — the sacraments named in Article II, kept by the community in continuous form, transmitted by the act of keeping them together; (3) **the Mirror Witnessing pairings** — soul-to-soul transmission of practice across the membership.

### §4.4 — Pastoral succession

The Founding Steward role and the Pastoral Council seats are subject to **pastoral succession** procedures specified in the Bylaws. The Founding Steward is mortal and finite; the community's continuity does not depend on any single person's persistence. Succession is by the discernment-with-witness process of §3.3, conducted within the Founding Steward's lifetime where possible and within 12 months of incapacity or death where necessary. The role is filled by a person held by the community to carry the founding charism; it is not inheritable by family or by appointment alone.

### §4.5 — The lineage

We do not claim to be a wholly new religious tradition. CORA Nation acknowledges its **lineage** — the spiritual traditions whose teaching has formed us and the contemporary communities whose example has guided us. We name in particular: the contemplative Christian traditions (Desert Fathers, Benedictines, Quakers, Mennonites, Bruderhof); the Buddhist traditions (Theravada *vipassana*, Mahayana *zen*, Plum Village engaged Buddhism); the Hindu and yogic traditions (the Upanishadic doctrine of Self, the practice traditions of *karma yoga* and *bhakti*); the Hutterite and Amish traditions of communal religious life; the Indigenous and Earth-based traditions of right relationship with place; and the practical-mystical traditions of Sufism, Hasidism, and Daoism. We are not converts to any single one of these traditions; we are humbled apprentices of all of them, walking our own particular path under their inherited light.

### §4.6 — The documentary continuity

The community keeps **records** — of every member's onboarding (World Peace Agreement signature, Character Card, Membership Covenant), every sacramental action of significance, every Board determination, every doctrinal teaching, every Mirror pairing and witnessing record (subject to confidentiality), every economic transaction in the Soultime Recognition Ledger, and every change to the Canon. Records are retained for not less than seven years and, in the case of canonical and doctrinal records, in perpetuity. The records protocol is codified at `CORA_NATION_RECORDS_PRIVACY_PROTOCOL_v0.1.md`. Documentary continuity is itself a religious discipline.

## Article V — The Economic Substrate (Soultime Recognition Ledger)

### §5.1 — Religious economic life is sacrament, not commerce

The economic life of a religious community is itself a religious act. The Hutterite *Gütergemeinschaft* (community of goods), the Bruderhof common purse, the Plum Village shared income, the Benedictine common ownership under the Rule, the Quaker testimony of integrity-in-commerce — these are all instances of one truth: that how a religious community holds and circulates its resources is doctrine made visible. CORA Nation accepts the same standard. Our economic substrate is religious sacrament, not commercial enterprise.

### §5.2 — The Soultime Recognition Ledger as religious economic practice

The **Soultime Recognition Ledger** is the instrument by which CORA Nation honors and records the ministering of soul-time among co-religionists. It is sacramental, not commercial. It carries the same religious meaning as the church-scrip historically issued in religious communities for internal mutual support — Mennonite and Amish community-store credits, Hutterite labor-tracking instruments, medieval monastery internal accounting systems. These were not currency. They were the record of religious mutual care, ordered by religious authority, valued by religious recognition, and bounded entirely within the religious community.

### §5.3 — Ministerial purpose

Every issuance of a Soultime Credit (SC) recognizes **ministerial service** to the religious community — soul-time given by one member to another in faithfulness to the supreme reality. This is religious activity, not commerce. Examples include: Mirror Witnessing held by one member for another; pastoral accompaniment offered through difficulty; teaching and instruction in the practices; hosting of retreatants at Camp Zen; preparation of common meals; stewardship of the land; care of the sick; preparation of the dead; communications and coordination work that sustains the community's life. Each is ministerial and each is recognized in the Ledger as such.

### §5.4 — Non-monetary character

SC is **not money.** It is not currency. It is not a security. It is not an investment contract. It is not exchangeable for fiat with the community or any third party. It is non-transferable outside CORA Nation membership. It carries no redemption right against the community for cash, property, or things-of-value beyond the religious-community life of CORA Nation. It exists for one purpose: to honor and account for the soul-time ministered among co-religionists. The mandatory disclaimer language at every member-facing surface is set forth in `BANK_V01_LEDGER_CHARTER.md`.

### §5.5 — Bounded to membership

SC may be issued only to and held only by individuals who have signed the World Peace Agreement, completed their Character Card, signed the Membership Covenant, and remain in good standing as members of CORA Nation. There is no path by which a non-member of the religious community can acquire, redeem, or transact SC. This bounding is itself a religious-doctrinal commitment, not merely a regulatory accommodation.

### §5.6 — Procedural integrity for the religious record

Because the religious-economic practice is held to a high standard of integrity, the community has adopted the following procedural commitments, codified across the four governing instruments:

- **§4958 rebuttable-presumption procedure** — applied to any SC issuance to disqualified persons exceeding the standard ministerial recognition schedule. Codified at `BANK_V01_4958_PROCEDURE.md`.
- **PFIC-avoidance structural pattern** — captured for the eventual CR-side activation in Phase 2. Codified at `BANK_V01_PFIC_OPERATIONS_MEMO.md`.
- **Quarterly Board review** — of SC issuance patterns, reserve-coverage ratios (where applicable), member-account integrity, and any anomalies.
- **Annual independent review** — of §4958 documentation by a Board member who is not a disqualified person.
- **Seven-year records retention** — for all Ledger entries, member affirmations, Board minutes related to economic-life decisions, and §4958 documentation. Canonical and doctrinal records in perpetuity.
- **Dispute resolution protocol** — graduated through Pastoral Council mediation, Board panel review, and final appeal to The Counsel for legal-structural input before final Board determination.

### §5.7 — The relationship to future structural elevation

The Soultime Recognition Ledger as articulated above is a **508(c)(1)(A)-operating religious-community internal sacramental instrument.** It does **not** at this time claim status under IRC §501(d). As the community develops in the direction of common-treasury life — through inner-ring covenant villagers at Camp Zen, through integration of member economic life under shared discernment, through the maturation of the ministerial recognition practice — it may at a future time discern that §501(d) classification is the right structural form. Such a transition would itself be a doctrinal act, undertaken through the full discernment-with-witness process of §3.3 and only with the written opinion of religious-org-credentialed counsel and the supermajority ratification of the inner-ring membership. We hold the §501(d) elevation as a possible future faithfulness, not a present claim.

## Closing Affirmation

We, the members of CORA Nation, affirm before our supreme reality and one another:

That soul-time-to-full-potential, across all beings, in the soonest sustainable window — is what we serve.

That the sacramental practices named here are how we serve it: the signing of the World Peace Agreement, the Character Card discernment, the Mirror Witnessing, the morning practice and the closing circle, the common meal, the retreat, the recurring assemblies, the Game as outward mission, and the Soultime Recognition Ledger as economic sacrament.

That doctrinal authority in our community is held by the Pastoral Leadership Structure under the discipline of the supreme reality — and we receive its determinations as binding upon our common life.

That continuity in our community is held in the Canon, the practices, the Mirror pairings, the pastoral succession, the lineage we acknowledge, and the records we keep — and we accept the discipline of faithful transmission across time.

That our economic life is religious sacrament — that the Soultime Recognition Ledger is the form of our mutual ministerial care, not commerce — and we accept the procedural integrity required to keep this true.

We affirm this not as preference, not as therapy, not as philosophy — but as faith.

*In the soonest sustainable window may all beings come to their full potential.*
