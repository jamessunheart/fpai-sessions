# Camp Zen — Financial Projections (CEO View)

**Frame:** James asked, "Show me your financial projections if you were running this?" — so this is what I'd model as if I were the CEO. Honest about variance, sensitivities, and what I'd watch month by month.

---

## TL;DR

**Run-rate at month 24:** $3.9M ARR (matches blueprint).
**Cumulative cash through 24 months:** ~$2.0M.
**24-month net (revenue minus operating costs):** ~+$500K cumulative.
**Cash you need to fund the gap:** ~$150K working capital in months 1–10.
**The linchpin:** Coherence Membership growth. The camp survives at $1.2–1.5M without it; the blueprint's $4M only happens if membership delivers.

The blueprint's targets are achievable but **the camp itself (Atlás's ownership zone — layers 2-4) sustains at ~$1.5M ARR**. Whether you hit $4M depends on layer 1 (Membership), which is your KPI not his. That's correct architecturally, but means Atlás's P&L success has a ceiling unrelated to him.

## 5. Sensitivity analysis

The projection is most sensitive to **membership growth** and **retreat occupancy**. Other layers are fairly predictable.

### Scenario A — Blueprint hits (1000 members by M24)
- Year 2 revenue: $1.56M
- Year 2 net: **+$735K**
- 24-mo ARR: $3.9M
- Cumulative net: **+$658K**

### Scenario B — Membership at 200 (not 1000)
- Year 2 membership rev: $237K (not $400K)
- Year 2 total revenue: $1.4M
- Year 2 net: **+$575K**
- 24-mo ARR: $3.4M
- Cumulative net: **+$498K**
- *Camp still very healthy; just below blueprint target*

### Scenario C — Membership goes to zero
- Year 2 revenue: $1.16M (just retreats + residency + anchor + B2B)
- Year 2 net: **+$335K**
- 24-mo ARR: $1.5M
- Cumulative net: **+$258K**
- *Camp survives, founder's $4M target doesn't hit*

### Scenario D — Retreat occupancy at 60% not 75%/90%
- Year 1 retreat rev: $144K (already at 60%, no change)
- Year 2 retreat rev: $288K (vs $360K)
- Year 2 net: **−$10K from above** in each scenario
- *Modest impact — retreats aren't the biggest lever*

### Scenario E — Property cost is $150K, not $50K
- Adds $100K/yr to OpEx
- Year 2 net: **+$635K** (still profitable)
- *Property carry is significant but not fatal*

### Worst-case combo (B + D + E)
- Year 2 revenue: $1.3M
- Year 2 OpEx: $920K
- Year 2 net: **+$380K**
- *Even in a bad scenario, the model works in year 2 if you fund the year-1 trough*

## 6. What I'd watch (CEO dashboard)

### Monthly leading indicators
1. **Membership net adds** — needs to track 5/mo year 1, 25/mo year 2 to hit ramp
2. **Retreat-week deposits** — booked weeks should always be 90+ days out; if drops below, signal
3. **Anchor Host pipeline** — need 4+ confirmed for next 2 quarters at all times
4. **Cash on hand** — runway in weeks; if <12 weeks, action

### Quarterly hard gates
1. **End of Q1 (month 3):** if membership <20, course-correct on funnel/AI infra. If retreat NPS <70, fix experience.
2. **End of Q2 (month 6):** if membership <50, the $4M target is at risk. Decide: double down on growth or rebase.
3. **End of Q4 (month 12):** if cumulative cash burn >$200K, restructure. If <$0K (i.e. positive), accelerate.

### Annual decisions
- Year 1 close: based on actual ramp, decide whether year 2 plan is the blueprint or a refined version.
- Year 2 close: if hitting $3.5M+ ARR, plan the second property. If at $1.5M ARR, consolidate.

## 7. CEO take — what I'd do differently

If I were running this, three calls I'd make differently from the blueprint as written:

### A. Soften the membership-or-bust framing
Blueprint targets 1,000 members at $197 in 24 months — that's a steep curve for a niche product (AI agents + community + content for spiritually-aligned, financially-able adults). It can happen. It also might not. **I'd plan for 300 members as the realistic midpoint** and treat 1,000 as upside. This shifts the camp's success metric from "did membership explode?" to "is the camp self-sustaining at $1.5M with membership upside?" — which is achievable on Atlás's execution alone.

### B. Front-load Anchor Host
This is the cleanest revenue per founder hour. Practitioners bring their own tribes. **I'd prioritize 6+ confirmed bookings in year 1, not 4.** The camp becomes infrastructure. Lower founder load, higher utilization, less marketing spend.

### C. CORA credits as customer-acquisition cost, not revenue
Residency partly in credits is brand-positive but cash-deferring. **I'd use credit-paid residency intentionally as customer acquisition** (residents become brand evangelists, fill membership, become Anchor Host candidates) and not load it onto the revenue line at face value. Treat credits as a marketing expense; cash flow analysis becomes cleaner.

## 9. Honest caveats

- **I don't know your existing financials.** If there's already $200K in the bank, the year-1 trough is non-issue. If you're at $0, the trough is the most urgent decision.
- **I assumed ramp curves are linear.** Real ramps are stair-step (slow then sudden). Could compress to year-2 hockey-stick or stretch to year-3.
- **B2B at $400K is founder-time-bound.** If you spend 40% of your time on B2B, this works. If your time goes to membership, B2B might be $200K.
- **Tax treatment varies wildly** depending on entity structure (US LLC, CR S.A., partnership). I haven't modeled tax — those numbers are pre-tax.
- **Currency risk:** if revenue is USD but costs are CRC (colón), exchange rate volatility matters. CR pegs softly to USD which helps.
